## Scope

Strain is a directional deformation relative to a stated reference structure. Epitaxial clamping, uniaxial loading and hydrostatic pressure impose different constraints, so “compressive” does not specify a unique path through a material's phase diagram. A thin film also carries substrate, defect and oxygen-history variables that a bulk pressure experiment does not reproduce automatically. [R1](#ref-source-363388aa03563c00) [R2](#ref-source-2c98c9257f2c4d46)

This article treats the mechanical control and its evidential limits. A changed transition temperature is an observation; the attribution of that change to a particular bond angle, orbital or fluctuation is a further inference. Strain is useful precisely because it can tune selected directions, but the experimental boundary conditions must be retained. [R1](#ref-source-363388aa03563c00) [R3](#ref-source-c9a97c501c718b62)

## Mathematical assumptions

For a lattice parameter $a$ relative to a relaxed reference $a_0$, an engineering strain is

$
\epsilon=\frac{a-a_0}{a_0}.
$

A nominal coherent epitaxial mismatch may be written

$
f=\frac{a_{\mathrm{sub}}-a_{\mathrm{ref}}}{a_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
$

The nominal mismatch is not automatically the measured strain in a partially relaxed film. Dislocations, domains and thickness-dependent relaxation can change the actual lattice parameters. State the reference phase, orientation and measurement temperature rather than assigning one strain percentage from a substrate name. [R1](#ref-source-363388aa03563c00)

In a small-deformation continuum description, the strain tensor is the symmetric displacement gradient. Hydrostatic stress has the form $\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}$, whereas uniaxial and epitaxial boundary conditions select directions. The resulting strain also depends on the elastic response. Out-of-plane expansion under in-plane compression is therefore not a contradiction. [R1](#ref-source-363388aa03563c00)

## Experimental criteria

Measure actual lattice deformation using an appropriate structural method and report calibration, loading direction, transverse response, relaxation and hysteresis. Keep nominal mismatch and measured strain in separate fields. For superconductivity, identify onset, zero resistance or a magnetic/thermodynamic criterion, and compare equivalent sample histories.

In Sr2RuO4, the earlier Hicks study and the later higher-strain Steppke study are distinct experimental steps. The larger transition-temperature peak belongs to the latter; it should not be attributed to the initial apparatus or to the earlier paper without checking the original curve. The pilot preserves an approximate source-reported scale while explicitly leaving an unretrieved criterion unknown. [R2](#ref-source-2c98c9257f2c4d46) [R3](#ref-source-c9a97c501c718b62)

## Limits and disputes

A peak in transition temperature near a reconstructed Fermi surface is a constraint on theory, not a determination of every pairing property. Orbital shifts, density-of-states changes and modified correlations can coexist. Inferring a pairing symmetry still requires complementary measurements. [R3](#ref-source-c9a97c501c718b62)

The pinned chapter's 2026 nickelate-film discussion distinguishes nominal and measured mismatch and warns against assuming that in-plane compression requires a shorter c axis. That update has not received a primary-text check here. Its strain percentages and transition values are consequently not promoted into the pilot's quantitative record. Oxygen stoichiometry, interface structure and defects remain possible covariates, not solved nuisance parameters. [R4](#ref-source-4b5b65a5047eb1a9)

## Discovery implications

Prefer controlled series that change one mechanical coordinate while monitoring oxygen, microstructure and sample geometry. A substrate series alone may change several variables at once. A useful design proposal predicts a trend, a null control and an independent structural or spectroscopic response, rather than stating that more compression must always improve superconductivity.
