## Scope

Chiral pairing describes a superconducting order with a handed orbital structure, such as a complex combination of two directional basis functions. Three questions must remain separate: whether time reversal is broken, whether the pairing is chiral, and whether a specified gapped Hamiltonian has a nonzero topological invariant. A Kerr signal, a complex coefficient or a named lattice does not answer all three. Kallin and Berlinsky's 2016 review is a historical guide; its candidate-material assignments are not current confirmations. [R2](#ref-source-69d33a869fbbb0c6)

Broken time reversal need not mean orbital chirality. For example, frustrated interband pair scattering can favor complex relative phases among otherwise s-wave gaps. Stanev and Tešanović studied this possibility in a three-band model. An illustrative vector of gaps on three inequivalent labelled bands, $(1,e^{2\pi i/3},e^{-2\pi i/3})$, cannot be made equal to its conjugate by one common phase, although each component has no angular winding. This statement assumes only a common U(1) freedom; it is not a stability calculation or a material assignment. [R8](#ref-source-3c7aa9b4c8da3abf)

## Mathematical assumptions

Read and Green distinguish weak- and strong-pairing phases of a two-dimensional spinless complex p-wave model. Their continuum chemical potential is measured relative to the band bottom: its zero marks a gap-closing transition in that model. The broken symmetry alone does not distinguish the two phases. This continuum criterion must not be copied unchanged into a lattice or multiband calculation. [R1](#ref-source-a33c639b7db4f05a)

For an independent lattice illustration, set hopping and pairing scales to one and use $H(\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}$, with $\mathbf{h}=(\sin k_x,\chi\sin k_y,-2\cos k_x-2\cos k_y-\mu)$ and $\chi=\pm1$. In this Nambu convention the off-diagonal gap is $\sin k_x-i\chi\sin k_y$. Define the occupied-band Berry connection as $\mathbf{A}=i\langle u_-|\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}u_-\rangle$. With the orientation $(k_x,k_y)$, the lower-band Chern number for $\chi=1$ is $0,-1,+1,0$ in the intervals separated by $\mu=-4,0,4$. Reversing $\chi$ reverses its sign. [R1](#ref-source-a33c639b7db4f05a) [R2](#ref-source-69d33a869fbbb0c6)

Sixteen calculations compare two mesh sizes, four chemical potentials and both handedness choices. Berry-link phases from explicit occupied eigenvectors agree with independently summed spherical solid angles. Four analytic gap-closing checks identify the excluded critical points; no gapped-band invariant is assigned there. The same complex gap can thus accompany a zero or nonzero Chern number. The calculation fixes the gap by hand and omits self-consistency, realistic bands and disorder. [R1](#ref-source-a33c639b7db4f05a)

A boundary calculation illustrates a different distinction. In Huang, Taylor and Kallin's ideal sharp-edge continuum treatment, occupied bound states and continuum states both contribute to the integrated current. For axial harmonic index $m=1$, their respective values are $1/2$ and $-1/4$ in units $\hbar k_F^2/(4\pi m^*)$ per spin component. For integer $m>1$, each integrated contribution vanishes in this limit despite the chiral edge branches. Independent angular quadrature reproduces the analytic sums for six values of $m$. [R3](#ref-source-25d6a80b561d5ad2)

That cancellation is not a universal absence of current in higher-order chiral pairing. The same paper shows that making the density vary slowly over distances much longer than the coherence length restores a current; lattice effects also matter. A sharp specular boundary, a smooth trap and a disordered multiband surface are different problems. The calculation above does not predict a magnetic-field amplitude for a real sample. [R3](#ref-source-25d6a80b561d5ad2)

## Experimental criteria

Kerr rotation probes an optical response. In the clean Galilean-invariant limit, a spatially uniform electric field couples to center-of-mass motion and the charge Hall response vanishes even for complex pairing. At the single-particle level, the clean one-band current-vertex structure also gives a cancellation; multiband and impurity processes require separate treatment. Optical frequency, refractive response and geometry matter. Neither a null Kerr measurement nor one nonzero angle can be converted directly into a Chern number. [R1](#ref-source-a33c639b7db4f05a) [R2](#ref-source-69d33a869fbbb0c6)

For UPt3, retain Schemm and colleagues' distinction between the Kerr onset near 0.46 K and the upper susceptibility onset near 0.55 K, including optical heating and field-training controls. For Sr2RuO4, Pustogow and colleagues' oxygen NMR response constrains a simple fixed-d-vector interpretation. The later 2026 muon Knight-shift study also has field-dependent extraction and low-temperature magnetization limits. These are complementary constraints, not a license to restore a historically favored chiral p-wave label as settled fact. [R4](#ref-source-d3edee404071ac81) [R5](#ref-source-c7f8b99a795aba41) [R9](#ref-source-c4434644191d0174)

Mielke and colleagues studied KV3Sb5, not the whole AV3Sb5 family interchangeably. Their selected manuscript shows an increased muon relaxation below the charge-order scale near 80 K that remains approximately constant across the superconducting transition near 1.1 K. They explicitly discuss electric-field-gradient contributions and use high-field measurements to support an electronic interpretation. The methods report residual-field compensation better than 30 mG; a 50 G longitudinal field decouples the relaxation. These are charge-order constraints, not a new superconducting chirality onset at 80 K. [R6](#ref-source-484da7abcc165cd3)

Chen and colleagues studied CsV3Sb5 with tunneling and Josephson spectroscopy. Their selected manuscript distinguishes bulk signatures near 2.8 K from a surface transition near 2.3 K, and uses a calibrated 300 mK electron temperature. Spatial gap and conductance modulations motivate their roton-PDW interpretation. The authors explicitly state that roton excitations were not directly observed. A proposed complex vortex–antivortex pattern is therefore an interpretation to test, not a measured uniform chiral d-wave order parameter. [R7](#ref-source-057c43c86174f961)

## Limits and disputes

Local magnitude alone does not resolve handedness: $|\Delta|^2=|\Delta^*|^2$. Four complex spatial examples verify this identity without making a tunneling-response model. A Josephson signal can support superconducting coherence, while determining orbital phase structure requires suitable reference geometry and controls. Fourier peaks, gap-depth maps and zero-bias peaks must retain their individual evidential roles. [R7](#ref-source-057c43c86174f961)

Do not require every time-reversal-breaking state to belong to a two-dimensional crystal representation. Complex combinations of distinct representations or interband phases can behave differently. Likewise, half-quantum vortices can occur in equal-spin-pairing states without establishing orbital chirality. A strain response or vortex signature must be interpreted in the actual symmetry and spin-orbit setting. [R2](#ref-source-69d33a869fbbb0c6) [R8](#ref-source-3c7aa9b4c8da3abf)

The local topology check is a fully gapped two-dimensional model. Nodal three-dimensional superconductors may instead require momentum-resolved invariants; a single integer cannot simply be carried over. Edge spectra, thermal transport, optical response and screened equilibrium charge currents require distinct calculations. Complete scientific acceptance, final-version comparisons, raw-data review and material-specific modelling remain open. [R1](#ref-source-a33c639b7db4f05a) [R2](#ref-source-69d33a869fbbb0c6) [R3](#ref-source-25d6a80b561d5ad2)

## Discovery implications

First identify the transition and its probe. Then specify the order-parameter basis, domain controls, bulk gap and Hamiltonian used for any topology claim. Compare positive and null evidence under compatible conditions, retaining charge-order and superconducting temperature scales separately. A useful discovery target is a state that satisfies these linked tests, not a material selected merely for a complex gap notation or a kagome lattice.
