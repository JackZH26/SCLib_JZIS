## Scope

A quantum spin liquid is not identified simply by an absence of detected magnetic order. A candidate material must be tested against valence-bond order, disorder, freezing and the finite temperature and time windows of the measurements. A spin gap can occur in different states; its observation alone does not identify topological order or fractionalized excitations. Here the organic Mott insulator κ-(BEDT-TTF)2Cu2(CN)3 illustrates how interpretations change when additional probes become available. [R2](#ref-source-9d15cd9236072f5c) [R4](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b) [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

RVB, or resonating valence bond, describes a family of theoretical constructions built from superpositions of singlet configurations. Anderson's cuprate proposal connects this idea to superconductivity in a doped correlated insulator; the abstract is a historical proposal, not a material-level proof of a spin-liquid parent. The allowed coverings, amplitudes and constraints must be specified. A generic U(1) construction is not automatically a Dirac state, and BCS mean-field language does not imply a phonon-only pairing interaction. [R1](#ref-source-5a6026d6455bf7a0)

## Mathematical assumptions

An independent finite example makes one logical limitation explicit. Consider an isolated singlet of energy zero and three triplet states with energies $\Delta-h$, $\Delta$ and $\Delta+h$. Use units $k_B=g\mu_B=1$, so temperatures, gaps and the field variable h are expressed in compatible energy units; the response below is not an SI molar susceptibility. The partition function is $Z=1+e^{-\Delta/T}[1+2\cosh(h/T)]$. Differentiating the mean moment at zero field gives $\chi_0=2e^{-\Delta/T}/\{T[1+3e^{-\Delta/T}]\}$. This illustrative model addresses an inference problem raised by the magnetic measurements, rather than fitting those experiments. [R2](#ref-source-9d15cd9236072f5c) [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

The model has a gap and a nonmagnetic singlet ground state, yet an isolated dimer has neither bulk topological order nor mobile charge. Adding a synthetic Curie contribution gives $\chi_{\mathrm{obs}}=\chi_0+c/T$. With an illustrative gap of 6 K, c = 0.001 and T = 0.1 K, the background exceeds 99.9999% of the total response even though the intrinsic response is exponentially suppressed. The chosen 6 K is a model parameter, not the measured anomaly temperature or a fitted material gap. An inverse-temperature tail cannot by itself establish a gapless intrinsic state. [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

Five finite-difference derivatives agree with the analytic response, and fifteen temperature/background combinations check this counterexample. Its Curie term assumes independent, unsaturated moments; interactions, anisotropy and finite-field saturation are excluded. Singlet correlations in an insulating model also do not demonstrate charge coherence or superconductivity. The separate [RVB discussion](/scwiki/rvb-and-doped-mott-insulators) examines wavefunction normalization and charge-response assumptions. These finite calculations are not a simulation of a bulk quantum spin liquid. [R1](#ref-source-5a6026d6455bf7a0) [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

## Experimental criteria

Shimizu and colleagues' lowest-temperature proton NMR measurements used a single crystal at 2.2 T perpendicular to the conducting planes, reaching 32 mK. That temperature floor is not a zero-field result. Their higher-temperature polycrystalline NMR used 3.9 T, and susceptibility measurements used 0.32 T. No resolved splitting or major broadening was observed at low temperature, but the small-moment estimate relies on calibration against a different chloride salt. The absence of a resolved signal therefore has a probe- and model-dependent bound. [R2](#ref-source-9d15cd9236072f5c)

Below approximately 0.4 K, the reported recovery needs two exponential components, with roughly linear and quadratic temperature dependences of their relaxation rates. The authors discuss spatial inhomogeneity and possible disorder-related explanations. Combining these components into one universal power law would discard relevant evidence. The approximately 250 K exchange scale obtained from the susceptibility analysis is neither the 32 mK measurement floor nor a superconducting transition temperature. [R2](#ref-source-9d15cd9236072f5c)

Miksch and colleagues separate a decreasing main ESR contribution below the approximately 6 K anomaly from narrow defect-related components. Their author manuscript uses conventional 9.5 GHz measurements near 340 mT down to 1.9 K, alongside separate low-frequency measurements near 40 mT reaching 25 mK. These are different protocols, not one continuous zero-field measurement. Samples from four laboratories show different defect signals. The proposed microscopic defect origin remains an interpretation; the final abstract describes a gapped ground state consistent with a valence-bond solid. [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

## Limits and disputes

Gap values require their observable and fitting model. Miksch's author supplement compares a simple activated fit giving 7.3 K with a chain-based fit giving 12.1 K. Its main text and figure legend include an inverse-temperature prefactor that the displayed supplementary expression omits; no unreported correction or independent reproduction of that fit is claimed here. Pustogow's final paper explicitly distinguishes its transport scale $E_g=2\Delta\approx30$–50 K from the approximately 12 K ESR spin gap. These numbers are not interchangeable measurements of one quantity, nor evidence by themselves of a manuscript-version error. [R4](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b) [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

The strain-transport study favors a valence-bond-solid interpretation but leaves a gapped chiral spin liquid possible. Its hydrostatic-equivalent pressure is inferred for a substrate-coupled crystal; it is not a directly applied hydrostatic pressure. Kurosaki's pressure phase diagram establishes neighboring electronic phases and superconductivity, but proximity does not identify the pairing mediator. Complete scientific acceptance remains open: final-version comparisons, raw-data analysis, full calibration and discrimination among microscopic states are incomplete. Reference labels identify which selected methods, figures or abstracts were actually checked. [R3](#ref-source-0443485ac20d3a8c) [R4](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b) [R5](#ref-source-ef796739e836bb78)

## Discovery implications

Record the sample, magnetic field, frequency or time window, temperature floor, background subtraction and fitting assumptions for each proposed spin-liquid signature. Compare magnetic, structural, thermodynamic and charge-coherence observations under compatible conditions. A useful mechanism proposal must predict observations that distinguish alternatives across the transition, including the behavior of defects. Superconductivity next to a candidate insulating state motivates such tests; it does not establish fractionalization-mediated pairing.
