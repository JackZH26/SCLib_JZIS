## Scope

A twist angle controls the relative orientation of two layers, changing the moiré pattern and the coupled electronic Hamiltonian. It does not independently specify carrier filling, interlayer tunneling, disorder, pairing interaction or superconducting topology. The original continuum model explains narrow bands and suppressed Dirac velocity at particular parameter values; observing a moiré pattern alone does not demonstrate a flat band or superconductivity. [R1](#ref-source-28797be9898b68b5)

Magic-angle graphene supplies experimentally accessible correlated phases, but an adjacent insulating state does not uniquely identify a one-band Mott mechanism or a common pairing mediator. The 2018 correlated-insulator and superconductivity papers are distinct sources, with different questions and evidence. Pressure-dependent superconductivity further shows why nominal angle cannot be treated as the only tuning parameter. ## Mathematical assumptions

For two matched, unstrained hexagonal lattices, define $a=0.246$ nm as graphene's lattice constant, **not** its carbon–carbon bond length. Then $L_m=a/[2\sin(\theta/2)]$, $A_m=\sqrt3L_m^2/2$ and $L_m\approx a/\theta$ for a small angle expressed in radians. Bistritzer–MacDonald's manuscript instead calls the bond length $a$, so its numerator is $\sqrt3a$: these conventions are equivalent. Using the bond length in the lattice-constant formula would underestimate the period by $\sqrt3$ and overestimate the inferred full-filling density by a factor of three. [R1](#ref-source-28797be9898b68b5) [R4](#ref-source-e80d36514bb4144d)

With four spin–valley states per moiré band, define the full-band density $n_s=4/A_m$ and filling $\nu=nA_m=4n/n_s$, measured relative to charge neutrality. At 1.10°, the ideal geometry gives $L_m\approx12.81$ nm and $n_s\approx2.81\times10^{12}$ cm⁻². This is a calculated reference, not a measured device value. The small-angle sensitivity is $\delta n_s/n_s\approx2\delta\theta/\theta$; the exact logarithmic derivative is $d\ln n_s/d\theta=\cot(\theta/2)$. A contact-averaged filling estimate cannot resolve a local twist/strain map, and broken-symmetry states need not retain fourfold degeneracy. [R1](#ref-source-28797be9898b68b5) [R4](#ref-source-e80d36514bb4144d)

Let $k_\theta=2K\sin(\theta/2)$, $K=4\pi/(3a)$, and $\alpha=w/(\hbar v_Fk_\theta)$, using wavevector units for $k_\theta$. In the first-shell approximation with the small layer-rotation correction neglected, $v_*/v_F=(1-3\alpha^2)/(1+6\alpha^2)$. Its zero at $\alpha=1/\sqrt3$ illustrates velocity cancellation, not an exact experimental magic angle. Tunneling, lattice relaxation and strain affect a realistic calculation. Our local check covers reciprocal geometry, density conversion and the stated algebraic zero; it does not diagonalize the complete continuum Hamiltonian or calculate a superconducting transition. [R1](#ref-source-28797be9898b68b5) [R4](#ref-source-e80d36514bb4144d)

For the Tanaka author-version circuit, distinguish the parallel termination inductance from the bulk MATBG branch: $L_{\parallel}^{-1}=L_{\rm prx}^{-1}+L_{\rm TBG}^{-1}$. This is not the inductance of the entire resonator. With active length $\ell$ and width $W$, the manuscript's $D_s=\ell/(W L_{\rm TBG})$ has units of inverse henries. In the phase-energy convention $F/A=J|\nabla\phi|^2/2$, the local conversion is $J=\hbar^2D_s/(4e^2)$, with $J$ an energy. Thus $D_s$ must not be compared numerically with an energy stiffness without converting units. A synthetic example with $L_{\rm TBG}=2.64$ nH and $L_{\rm prx}=1.32$ nH gives $L_{\parallel}=0.88$ nH; attributing this whole parallel response to the bulk would overestimate its stiffness threefold. These are an illustrative calculation and a source-based circuit parameter, not a measured bulk inductance. Active aspect ratio and branch separation remain model assumptions; an incompatible subtraction does not establish a negative physical stiffness. [R5](#ref-source-be7282623ca96056)

## Experimental criteria

Cao's published superconductivity paper labels M1 at 1.16° and M2 at 1.05°. Figure 2 assigns maximum half-normal-resistance transition temperatures of about 0.5 K to M1 and 1.7 K to M2; the figure and caption resolve the device assignment. Both reached the reported zero-resistance state at the 70 mK measurement floor. A separate tentative cubic current–voltage fit for M2 at $n=-1.44\times10^{12}$ cm⁻² gave $T_{\rm BKT}\approx1.0$ K. The midpoint, inferred BKT scale and measurement floor must remain separate records. [R3](#ref-source-90c67b111a6e919c)

Yankowitz's nominal 1.27° device D2 developed superconducting transport under hydrostatic pressure. The published pressure plot defines $T_c$ by the intersection of low- and high-temperature linear fits, and the reported resistance falls to a roughly 10-ohm noise floor. The author supplement lists approximately 3.1 K at 1.33 GPa and 1.7 K at 2.21 GPa; the 300 mK base temperature is separate. These fitted transition temperatures should not be relabeled as exact zero-resistance or half-resistance thresholds. The same supplement reports removal, solvent cleaning and reloading between these pressures, which adds a possible disorder change to the pressure comparison. [R4](#ref-source-e80d36514bb4144d)

The same study's ambient-pressure D1, at approximately 1.14°, had fitted transition scales near 0.4 K for holes and 0.25 K for electrons, with a 10 mK base temperature. Different contacts showed different full-filling densities; the final text reports moiré unit-cell-area variations up to roughly 30% within a device. The authors used full-filling features and quantum-oscillation extrapolations to estimate angle. They did not establish a uniform angle at every point merely by quoting two decimal places. [R4](#ref-source-e80d36514bb4144d)

Tanaka and colleagues' 2025 study combines microwave response and transport. The checked author v2 calibrates a proximitized-edge branch using an insulating-state resistance assumption; strong normal-metal damping limits resonance extraction. Its conventional-stiffness comparison uses a Fermi velocity inferred from nonlinear transport, not an independent band-velocity measurement. Refrigerator base (20 mK), effective device temperature (about 40 mK), and transition temperatures are different quantities. Supplementary Figure 12 defines onset by intersecting two fitted lines; the half-resistance criterion crosses half the normal-line intercept at zero temperature, and the “zero” criterion extrapolates the falling line to zero. The approximately 1 K half-resistance scale is therefore not a universal measured-zero threshold. The comparison with base-temperature stiffness does not directly measure the renormalized BKT jump. Geometric and gap interpretations remain model-dependent; neither a unique pairing mediator nor a model-independent geometric fraction follows. [R5](#ref-source-be7282623ca96056)

## Limits and disputes

Compare local geometry, filling calibration, contacts and thermal history before interpreting differences between devices. Pressure-dependent transition curves, a nearby insulator or a broadened dome are not unique fingerprints of a pairing mechanism. Distinguish measured resistance, nonlinear response and stiffness from a proposed microscopic explanation. Likewise, normal-state band topology does not alone fix the topology of the superconducting quasiparticles. Transferring graphene conclusions to a lattice-mismatched semiconductor stack requires a new model and platform-specific evidence. ## Discovery implications

Optimize angle together with tunneling, strain, filling and phase stiffness. Store each transition under its device, carrier branch, pressure and criterion; keep measurement floors separate. Use local imaging and contact-resolved transport to test uniformity. A useful design can combine favorable pairing and adequate phase coherence, but narrow bandwidth alone is not a sufficient target or a universal guarantee of higher $T_c$.
