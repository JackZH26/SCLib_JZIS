## Scope

Two-dimensional geometry and interface location are different coordinates. A film can have weak out-of-plane electronic dispersion without displaying a clean two-dimensional phase transition. An interface can host a superconducting region even when its two constituents are not superconducting alone. Neither statement identifies one universal mechanism for increasing a critical temperature. [R1](#ref-source-ea5d0c80335c450d) [R2](#ref-source-684b96e9dbb65a64)

An interface may change charge transfer, oxygen defects, strain, orbital structure, phonons and disorder at the same time. Pair formation and macroscopic phase coherence can also occur at different scales. A useful report states whether a temperature comes from a spectral gap, resistive onset, zero resistance, magnetic screening or a BKT analysis. [R4](#ref-source-a95bcf0cb2681557) [R5](#ref-source-f9fe6e9bc3792dcc) [R6](#ref-source-8f81510a80c35e3a)

## Mathematical assumptions

For an ideal two-dimensional phase model, the BKT universal jump can be written in an energy-stiffness convention as

$
k_BT_{\mathrm{BKT}}=\frac{\pi}{2}J_s(T_{\mathrm{BKT}}^-).
$

Here $J_s$ is the renormalized total phase stiffness immediately below the transition. The normalization and the relevant long-wavelength limit must be stated. The relation does not apply directly to a bare mean-field pairing amplitude, and it does not separately measure a geometric contribution to the total stiffness. Finite size, inhomogeneity and interlayer coupling can modify an experimental comparison. [R6](#ref-source-8f81510a80c35e3a)

BCS-like amplitude formation and BKT-like phase ordering describe different aspects of a system. Treating them as mutually exclusive microscopic pairing mechanisms would confuse an interaction model with a phase-coherence transition. [R6](#ref-source-8f81510a80c35e3a)

## Experimental criteria

Combine independent phase-sensitive observables rather than fitting one resistive tail. Test non-linear current-voltage behavior, a suitable fluctuation-transport regime and sheet stiffness where available. Check finite-size effects, heating, current range and measurement frequency. A large anisotropy of the upper critical field is not by itself a BKT proof.

At LaAlO3/SrTiO3, Reyren and colleagues reported an interface transition around 200 mK with two-dimensional behavior. The source's inferred superconducting thickness is an upper bound, not an exact directly imaged electronic width. Gating can tune an interface phase diagram without proving that carrier density is its only control parameter. [R1](#ref-source-ea5d0c80335c450d)

## Interface examples

Cuprate heterostructures constructed from non-superconducting components demonstrate that an interface can support superconductivity. Gozar and colleagues and the layer-selective work of Logvenov and colleagues constrain where the superconducting response occurs. They establish particular interface states rather than the claim that interlayer coupling is irrelevant to every high-temperature cuprate. [R2](#ref-source-684b96e9dbb65a64) [R3](#ref-source-7fcd7faadf66481b)

For single-layer FeSe/SrTiO3, a roughly 65 ± 5 K ARPES pairing scale retains its spectroscopic label. Replica-band evidence supports an interface electron-phonon interaction, but does not quantify every contribution to the full pairing kernel. This number cannot satisfy a query requiring zero resistance, even though it is a valid temperature observation. [R4](#ref-source-a95bcf0cb2681557) [R5](#ref-source-f9fe6e9bc3792dcc)

## Limits and disputes

A dome-shaped gate dependence does not uniquely identify a quantum critical pairing interaction. Ionic gating also needs electrochemical controls. A superconducting single layer shows that one layer can suffice under the reported conditions, not that dimensionality or interlayer coupling never matters. Interface improvements should not be merged with bulk or transient temperature records. [R1](#ref-source-ea5d0c80335c450d) [R3](#ref-source-7fcd7faadf66481b) [R6](#ref-source-8f81510a80c35e3a)

## Discovery implications

Plan controls that separate thickness, interface termination, oxygen, strain and electrostatics. Report sample-to-sample variation alongside the most favorable device. A mechanism becomes more useful when it predicts which independent interface modification should change a gap scale and which should change phase stiffness. Keep those two outcomes separate in both the narrative and structured evidence. [R5](#ref-source-f9fe6e9bc3792dcc) [R6](#ref-source-8f81510a80c35e3a)
