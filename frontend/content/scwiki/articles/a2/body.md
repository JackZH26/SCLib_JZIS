## Scope

The Meissner effect concerns the magnetic state selected on entering superconductivity. Meissner and Ochsenfeld's historical observation of flux expulsion is conceptually different from merely freezing the field already inside an ideal perfect conductor. Electrical transport and magnetic response therefore answer complementary questions. A sample may screen a changing field while retaining trapped vortices, so the cooling protocol must accompany a magnetization curve. [R1](#ref-source-512705f3e64d88d7) [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4)

Separate zero-field-cooled shielding, field-cooled magnetization and magnetic hysteresis. Strong pinning can make the field-cooled signal small even in a superconducting specimen. Conversely, a large shielding response cannot be read as an exact superconducting volume fraction without considering shape, connectivity and background subtraction. The magnetic inference belongs to the actual sample and protocol, not to a chemical formula in isolation. [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4)

## Mathematical assumptions

The local, homogeneous London description gives

$
\nabla^2\mathbf B=\frac{\mathbf B}{\lambda_L^2}.
$

The penetration depth $\lambda_L$ describes the decay of magnetic field in the stated limit. In a simple single-component convention, $\lambda_L^{-2}$ is proportional to superfluid weight. Multiband, anisotropic or non-local electrodynamics requires a more careful response model; the same fitted depth need not represent the same microscopic density across those settings. [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4)

For an approximately ellipsoidal specimen, the measured susceptibility is altered by a demagnetizing field. The correction depends on the field direction and shape. Quoting a shielding percentage without the underlying convention can produce values that look like a volume fraction but do not have that interpretation. The pilot therefore records the measurement criterion separately from any transition temperature. [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4)

## Experimental criteria

Report specimen dimensions, orientation, applied field, cooling protocol, field history and background treatment. State whether the result is a shielding estimate, field-cooled expulsion, local magnetic response, penetration depth or sheet stiffness. Bulk claims benefit from consistency with transport and thermodynamic measurements rather than one isolated diamagnetic trace.

Low-temperature penetration-depth behavior constrains low-energy quasiparticles. Hardy and colleagues' YBCO microwave work is an important cuprate example, but extracting a gap structure still depends on impurity scattering, surface effects and the electrodynamic limit. A power law by itself does not uniquely identify an irreducible pairing representation. [R3](#ref-source-b7bc48c845a47b53)

## Limits and disputes

Geometry, granularity and pinning can separate shielding from equilibrium flux expulsion. A small magnetic signal may reflect a small superconducting region, a difficult geometry or background uncertainty; these possibilities must be evaluated rather than silently choosing one. Likewise, magnetic screening alone does not identify a pairing mediator. [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4)

Superfluid-density studies of overdoped La2-xSrxCuO4 constrain how the condensate evolves across a controlled sample series. Such correlations are useful but do not automatically determine a microscopic interaction. When comparing stiffness across two- and three-dimensional systems, retain thickness and the electromagnetic normalization. A geometric contribution cannot be obtained merely by relabeling a measured total stiffness. [R4](#ref-source-b96ffab89d45664d)

## Discovery implications

A persuasive magnetic evidence package documents what field is excluded, which part is trapped and how the susceptibility was corrected. Pair transport and magnetic probes on comparable samples. For thin films, explicitly report whether the inferred quantity is a sheet response or a bulk penetration depth. These distinctions support reproducibility without turning an inversion model into an unqualified material property. [R2](#ref-source-0590b237a1d87de4) [R4](#ref-source-b96ffab89d45664d)
