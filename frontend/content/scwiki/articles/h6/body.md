## Scope

Electronic nematic order makes symmetry-related directions inequivalent without requiring a new translation period. For a tetragonal crystal, a common example is spontaneous fourfold-to-twofold symmetry reduction. Resistivity anisotropy is a possible response, not a universal definition of the order parameter: external strain, pre-existing orthorhombicity and domain populations can also make transport anisotropic. Establishing spontaneous order requires identifying the symmetry-breaking field and its zero-field limit. [R1](#ref-source-bd2e7b2930bdd775) [R2](#ref-source-bac80c20c1347e23) [R3](#ref-source-d29c3c764d26edfd)

Electronic and lattice degrees of freedom with the same symmetry couple. Evidence for an electronic instability does not make the lattice irrelevant, and the small size of a distortion alone does not prove which subsystem drives the transition. Orbital polarization, unequal fluctuations at two magnetic wave vectors and structural strain are candidate coupled variables; their appearance together is not a causal ranking. [R1](#ref-source-bd2e7b2930bdd775) [R5](#ref-source-643062e6ad91fafe)

## Mathematical assumptions

A uniform illustrative free energy is $F=a\phi^2/2+b\phi^4/4+C_0\epsilon^2/2-\lambda\phi\epsilon-\sigma\epsilon$, with nematic variable $\phi$, symmetry-matched strain $\epsilon$ and stress $\sigma$. In the stable symmetric regime, the relaxed modulus is $C=C_0-\lambda^2/a$. At fixed strain, $\partial\phi/\partial\epsilon=\lambda/a$; stress control instead allows the lattice to relax. For $a=A(T-T_0)$, a continuous mean-field coupled instability occurs at $T_s=T_0+\lambda^2/(AC_0)$. The extrapolated bare scale $T_0$ is neither automatically the measured transition nor a superconducting temperature. Positive quartic stabilization and explicit strain conventions are required. [R3](#ref-source-d29c3c764d26edfd) [R5](#ref-source-643062e6ad91fafe)

Band splitting also needs a symmetry test. At a symmetry point, the independent two-orbital illustration $H=\lambda_{\rm so}\tau_y\sigma_z+\phi\tau_z$ has energies $E_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm so}^2+\phi^2}$, each twice degenerate. Pauli matrices $\tau$ and $\sigma$ act on orbital and spin space. The spin–orbit term can preserve fourfold and time-reversal symmetries even when the bands are split; the nematic term reverses sign under a quarter rotation. Thus an observed splitting alone is not a symmetry-breaking diagnosis. This minimal model omits realistic dispersions and is not a fit to FeSe. [R7](#ref-source-82e36852ee3c27b9)

For pre-existing nematic order, symmetry can permit $\phi(\Delta_s^*\Delta_d+\mathrm{c.c.})$ when the chosen d-wave component transforms like $\phi$. In the illustrative quadratic matrix with diagonal entries $a_s(T-T_{s0})$, $a_d(T-T_{d0})$ and real off-diagonal $h=g\phi$, the highest instability is $T_c=(T_{s0}+T_{d0})/2+\sqrt{(T_{s0}-T_{d0})^2/4+h^2/(a_sa_d)}$, for positive slopes. The mixed gap has a common onset; anisotropic gap magnitude or critical fields do not imply separate a-axis and b-axis thermodynamic transition temperatures. Quartic terms can permit additional lower-temperature phases, which this calculation does not solve. [R10](#ref-source-03c03feac2435b1b)

## Experimental criteria

Chu's detwinned Co-substituted Ba122 crystals show larger resistivity along the shorter orthorhombic b axis. Above the structural transition, applied uniaxial stress already induces anisotropy, consistent with a substantial susceptibility. That induced response must not be relabelled spontaneous high-temperature order. Differential elastoresistance adds a strain-response measurement, with geometric backgrounds, strain transfer and residual bias requiring control. [R2](#ref-source-bac80c20c1347e23) [R3](#ref-source-d29c3c764d26edfd)

Böhmer's three-point bending experiment measures a normalized Young modulus along tetragonal [110]. Inferring the shear modulus uses an approximation, a noncritical background and normalization choices. Its thermodynamic treatment cannot by itself distinguish spin-driven from orbital-driven order. The Co- and K-substituted series show different Curie–Weiss behavior; the study does not establish an identical quantum critical point beneath every superconducting dome. [R5](#ref-source-643062e6ad91fafe)

Shimojima's FeSe ARPES assigns a roughly 50 meV zone-corner separation to orbital order in detwinned samples. The experiment uses different photon energies and resolutions for zone-center, zone-corner and detwinned measurements; these are not interchangeable spectra. Watson's later zone-center study resolves a spin–orbit contribution above the structural transition and additional splitting below it. Its constrained peak fits have larger uncertainty for a band partly above the Fermi cutoff. Momentum location, orbital assignment and fitting assumptions must accompany each energy scale. [R6](#ref-source-43471709790b0a92) [R7](#ref-source-82e36852ee3c27b9)

FeSe also separates static magnetism from dynamical evidence. Böhmer's NMR interpretation and Wang's later finite-energy neutron results sample different frequency windows. The absence of conventional ambient-pressure magnetic order does not exclude spin fluctuations or settle the microscopic nematic driver. Neither a structural onset nor an ARPES measurement temperature is a superconducting transition record. [R8](#ref-source-229de7733ef6e2b5) [R9](#ref-source-3686ada319d8a30c)

Cuprates require their own controls. Ando reports anisotropic transport in untwinned crystals and interprets it in terms of conducting stripes. Lawler analyzes intra-unit-cell STM contrast near the pseudogap energy, with oxygen-site differences and precise lattice registration. This is not an ARPES image of Fermi arcs. Existing lattice anisotropy, chain contributions where present, spatial inhomogeneity and charge modulation must be assessed before inferring a distinct bulk nematic transition from any one signal. [R11](#ref-source-34e188caa8191123) [R12](#ref-source-b70aa455468dbc62)

## Limits and disputes

A susceptibility enhancement or extrapolated Curie–Weiss scale supports a proposed instability within its analysis model; it is not direct observation of an uncovered zero-temperature critical point. A superconducting dome near that scale does not establish the pairing interaction. Controlled theories of nematic fluctuation enhancement remain distinct from a unique material-level mechanism, and composition-dependent deviations must stay visible. [R3](#ref-source-d29c3c764d26edfd) [R13](#ref-source-24dd77640a555a0f) [R14](#ref-source-f9ae22fe4f6ed654)

Complete scientific acceptance remains open. Chu's correction text still needs primary verification. Selected author manuscripts require final-version comparisons, including Watson's abstract/body difference in the quoted splitting increase and Shimojima's transition labels. Full strain calibration, ARPES fitting and STM registration have not been independently reproduced. No precise transition record is extracted from unresolved version differences. ## Discovery implications

Combine symmetry-resolved strain response with structural, magnetic and momentum-resolved measurements on documented samples. Track domain history, mechanical boundary conditions, energy resolution and the reference axes used for symmetry-channel names. Predictions should specify which measurement distinguishes a primary instability from a coupled response, and whether the proposed tuning changes static order, fluctuations or both. Keep proposed pairing benefits separate from demonstrated superconducting transition shifts.
