## Scope

Multiband pairing concerns how superconducting gaps on distinct bands are coupled. Multichannel pairing concerns different interaction contributions to a Cooper-pair kernel. Neither concept is equivalent to parallel normal-state electrical conductivities, and neither means that two spatially separate superconducting phases form one homogeneous condensate. Keeping these distinctions explicit prevents apparent evidence for several channels from becoming an unsupported mechanism claim. ## Mathematical assumptions

For a simplified weak-coupling model with a shared cutoff energy $E_c$, a linearized gap equation can be written

$
\Delta_i=L(T_c)\sum_j\lambda_{ij}\Delta_j,
\qquad
L(T_c)=\ln\!\left(\frac{1.13E_c}{k_BT_c}\right).
$

In this declared approximation, the largest relevant pairing eigenvalue sets the instability. Off-diagonal entries represent interband pair transfer and help constrain relative gap phases. A matrix eigenvalue is not the arithmetic sum of all its entries. The energy cutoff notation here makes the dimensions explicit when converting the original ASCII equation. [R1](#ref-source-e6ac5c48a0e11fe3)

The full problem can have different frequency spectra, momentum structure, repulsive terms, Coulomb effects and normal self-energy renormalization. Combining a pairing attraction and a mass enhancement into one positive scalar can give a misleading temperature estimate. Interactions can cooperate, compete or favor different gap structures after projection into the pairing channel. [R3](#ref-source-7c4eac077ae5e221)

## Experimental criteria

Resolve gap magnitudes and their band assignments with complementary spectroscopy and thermodynamics. State the band or orbital basis, energy cutoff, Coulomb treatment and disorder model. Distinguish a measurement that observes two gaps from an inference that identifies two different mediating bosons. Normal-state two-component Drude fits do not by themselves establish two superconducting gaps.

The MgB2 discovery paper reports superconductivity near 39 K. The detailed sample and transition criterion have not been re-extracted in this review, so the material record keeps its criterion explicitly unknown. [R2](#ref-source-fade6558e7eafb2b)

Choi and colleagues calculated band-dependent gaps in MgB2 using an anisotropic Eliashberg treatment of electron-phonon pairing. Their sigma- and pi-band gaps vanish at a common calculated transition temperature. This is a theoretical result consistent with multiple gaps within that model; it is not a direct measurement of two independent pairing mediators. [R3](#ref-source-7c4eac077ae5e221)

## Material interpretation

In single-layer FeSe/SrTiO3, replica-band observations support the relevance of an interface electron-phonon interaction. They do not independently extract every spin, interface and plasmon contribution as a set of additive coupling constants. The interface hypothesis must be tested together with charge transfer, the reconstructed electronic structure and phase-coherence limitations. [R4](#ref-source-f9fe6e9bc3792dcc)

A high pairing or gap-closing scale measured by ARPES is not automatically a zero-resistance transition. When an interface and intrinsic electronic interactions are both discussed, the useful object is a specified combined kernel with assumptions and uncertainties. This pilot deliberately contains no unsourced table of separate FeSe/SrTiO3 coupling constants. [R4](#ref-source-f9fe6e9bc3792dcc)

## Limits and disputes

A common transition temperature is compatible with interband locking, but does not establish beneficial synergy between distinct interactions. Changes in a band structure, density of states or scattering rate can alter the instability without revealing a unique mediator. Cross-material percentages assigned to a plasmon enhancement are not universal physical constants. [R1](#ref-source-e6ac5c48a0e11fe3) [R3](#ref-source-7c4eac077ae5e221)

## Discovery implications

A testable multichannel proposal predicts a gap structure and its response to an independent control parameter. Show what would distinguish cooperation from competition, and check that the same interaction has not been counted twice. The most useful next measurement constrains a specific matrix element or spectral contribution rather than simply adding another possible mechanism to a list.
