## Scope

The quantum metric measures how a normalized quantum state changes with parameters after removing its unobservable overall phase. For Bloch bands, those parameters can be crystal momentum. It describes wavefunctions rather than only energy dispersion. A flat band can therefore have nonzero metric, while a large metric alone neither supplies an attractive interaction nor establishes superconductivity. Provost and Vallée developed a general state-manifold construction; later multiband superconductivity work connected band geometry to current response under specified assumptions. [R1](#ref-source-fbbc7a50ad47a3f8) [R2](#ref-source-fdf091e09e7f4a6f) [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63)

This page uses a single isolated band or a specified smooth band subspace. It does not identify a single-particle Bloch metric with every many-body fidelity susceptibility. Keep the Hilbert space, parameter coordinates, orbital basis and selected subspace explicit. A geometric quantity can vary continuously without becoming a quantized topological invariant. ## Mathematical assumptions

For a normalized state $|u\rangle$, let $P=|u\rangle\langle u|$ and $Q_{ij}=\langle\partial_i u|(1-P)|\partial_j u\rangle$. We define $g_{ij}=\mathrm{Re}\,Q_{ij}$ and $\Omega_{ij}=-2\mathrm{Im}\,Q_{ij}$, consistent with $A_i=i\langle u|\partial_i u\rangle$. Thus $Q_{ij}=g_{ij}-i\Omega_{ij}/2$. The nearby-state fidelity obeys $1-|\langle u(\mathbf k)|u(\mathbf k+d\mathbf k)\rangle|^2=g_{ij}dk_i dk_j+O(|d\mathbf k|^3)$. With physical wavevectors, the metric components have units of length squared; dimensionless lattice coordinates change those units. [R1](#ref-source-fbbc7a50ad47a3f8) [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63)

For a rank-one projector, $Q_{ij}=\mathrm{Tr}[P(\partial_iP)(\partial_jP)]$ and $g_{ij}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}[(\partial_iP)(\partial_jP)]$. The projector is unchanged by $|u\rangle\rightarrow e^{i\phi(\mathbf k)}|u\rangle$, so this construction removes phase-gauge artifacts. For an isolated group of touching bands, use the smooth subspace projector and state the trace convention. A chosen individual eigenvector can become singular or ambiguous at a degeneracy. Phase-gauge invariance within a fixed orbital representation is not permission to ignore a momentum-dependent change of the underlying basis. [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63) [R5](#ref-source-72f7ddb9f13bbd81)

Positivity of the two-coordinate matrix $Q$ gives $\det g\geq\Omega_{xy}^2/4$ and $g_{xx}+g_{yy}\geq|\Omega_{xy}|$. The trace statement refers to the selected common coordinate convention. For the spinor $u=(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\varphi}\sin(\theta/2))$, our convention gives $g_{\theta\theta}=1/4$, $g_{\varphi\varphi}=\sin^2\theta/4$ and $\Omega_{\theta\varphi}=-\sin\theta/2$. This two-level example saturates the determinant inequality; a three-level family can satisfy it strictly. These are model checks, not measurements of a material. [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63)

Normalization must be translated before comparing formulas. Liang's checked manuscript defines $\mathcal R_{ij}=2\mathrm{Tr}[P(\partial_iP)(\partial_jP)]$ and calls its real part the metric. Consequently $g^{\rm Liang}=2g$ in this page's convention, and that paper writes the fidelity distance with an extra factor of one half. Its curvature sign must likewise be translated consistently. This is a convention difference, not a contradiction or a factor-of-two enhancement of a physical stiffness. [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63)

As a counterexample to equating metric with topology, take $u=(\cos(k_x/2),\sin(k_x/2))$, independent of $k_y$. The projector is periodic on a $2\pi$ torus, $g_{xx}=1/4$, and the Berry curvature vanishes. The Hamiltonian $H=E_0(2P-I)$ has flat eigenvalues $\pm E_0$ for positive constant $E_0$. It has nonzero metric and zero Chern number without introducing pairing. Our finite-difference checks compare projector derivatives, phase-transformed states and symmetric fidelity curvature; they do not calculate a superconducting response. [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63)

## Experimental criteria

An experimental response may constrain band geometry through a model and matrix elements; it need not uniquely reconstruct every metric component. For superconductivity, distinguish the measured total stiffness from a decomposition into conventional and geometric terms. Report carrier filling, band window, temperature, units and the response-to-metric conversion. A successful fit does not by itself identify a unique microscopic pairing mechanism. [R3](#ref-source-fa7a165c8c344a63) [R4](#ref-source-be7282623ca96056)

Tanaka and colleagues' 2025 MATBG study combines microwave kinetic inductance and transport. The checked author version includes a parallel edge-proximity circuit and an assumed active-region aspect ratio. It distinguishes a 20 mK refrigerator base from an approximately 40 mK wired-device temperature, and a half-normal-resistance transition near 1 K across much of the gate range. These are different quantities. Its comparison supports a connection to quantum geometry without directly isolating a model-independent geometric fraction. This page stores the 40 mK value only as a measurement condition. [R4](#ref-source-be7282623ca96056)

## Limits and disputes

Peotta–Törmä's flat-band result assumes an interaction window between the bandwidth and separating band gaps, together with an appropriate uniform-pairing construction. Liang extends the response analysis, but the isolated-band expression retains time-reversal, orbital-uniform pairing and band-separation assumptions. Huhtinen and colleagues subsequently identify missing order-parameter-response terms in earlier formulas and connect isolated-flat-band stiffness to a minimal quantum metric. Therefore an arbitrary raw metric integral should not be presented as an automatically complete quantitative stiffness prediction. ## Discovery implications

Calculate geometry with a reproducible orbital convention and subspace, check mesh convergence and preserve normalization factors. Test pairing and total phase stiffness separately. A topological lower bound can be useful under its assumptions, but zero Chern number does not exclude useful geometry, and nonzero metric does not guarantee superconductivity. Geometry is one constraint in a material-specific calculation, alongside interactions, filling, disorder and phase coherence.
