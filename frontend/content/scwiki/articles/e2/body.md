## Scope

Chemical doping changes a material's composition; it does not supply a universal carrier counter. Distinguish the starting mixture, measured site occupancy, formal ionic charge and mobile carriers. Substitution can also change disorder, bond geometry and competing order. Oxygen removal or chain ordering adds another control variable. A superconducting dome is therefore a relationship among specified samples and criteria, not a recipe transferable between families. Notation matters even before measurement. Takagi's 1989 paper uses $(\mathrm{La}_{1-x}\mathrm{Sr}_x)_2\mathrm{CuO}_4$ and studies $x\leq0.175$. In the alternative formula $\mathrm{La}_{2-y}\mathrm{Sr}_y\mathrm{CuO}_4$, the same composition has $y=2x$, reaching 0.35. The inherited draft attached 0.175 to the latter convention without converting it. This page corrects that factor-of-two mismatch; the original abstract, not its inaccessible full methods, was checked. [R1](#ref-source-5030a5999bf0420a)

## Mathematical assumptions

Charge neutrality is useful bookkeeping under explicit ionic assumptions. Taking La/Nd as $3+$, Sr as $2+$, Ce as $4+$ and O as $2-$ gives formal average Cu valence $2+x+2\delta$ in $\mathrm{La}_{2-x}\mathrm{Sr}_x\mathrm{CuO}_{4+\delta}$, and $2-x-2y$ in $\mathrm{Nd}_{2-x}\mathrm{Ce}_x\mathrm{CuO}_{4-y}$. Stoichiometric infinite-layer $\mathrm{Nd}_{1-x}\mathrm{Sr}_x\mathrm{NiO}_2$ gives formal Ni valence $1+x$. These balances do not locate holes among orbitals or establish an itinerant carrier density. Oxygen deviations and compensating defects change the balance. [R1](#ref-source-5030a5999bf0420a) [R5](#ref-source-c636cc691b7ff013)

Even the elementary weak-field two-band Drude model yields $R_H=(n_h\mu_h^2-n_e\mu_e^2)/[e(n_h\mu_h+n_e\mu_e)^2]$, with $e>0$. Mobilities weight the response. For compensation, $n_e=n_h=n$, this becomes $(\mu_h-\mu_e)/[en(\mu_h+\mu_e)]$; its sign can reverse at fixed density. This illustrative identity is not a fit to correlated transport. Kasahara explicitly discusses why that simple model fails for the magnitude of the observed Hall response. [R4](#ref-source-b9e77d2ee024ff3b)

Liang's empirical YBCO calibration uses $p=11.491u+5.17\times10^9u^6$, $u=1-c/c_0$, with $c_0=1.18447\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $c$ measured at 22°C. It depends on chain-order assumptions and reference compositions. The relation $T_c/T_{c,\max}=1-82.6(p-0.16)^2$ has two algebraic branches and fails near the material's suppressed one-eighth region. Neither expression is a universal oxygen-to-hole conversion; a fitted lattice parameter and a directly measured carrier density are different evidence. [R2](#ref-source-e24ef58054ac9b71)

## Experimental criteria

Measure composition on the specimens whose transitions are compared. Ni's Co-substituted Ba122 series used wavelength-dispersive spectroscopy (WDS): actual Co fraction was about 0.74 times the nominal fraction for that growth series. A nominal 0.10 batch averaged approximately 0.074. Its phase diagram distinguishes resistance, magnetization and heat capacity. Resistive offset means maximum-slope extrapolation to zero, rather than an independently established instrumental zero. The reported maximum near 23 K spans measured fractions approximately 0.058–0.074. [R3](#ref-source-cf1286d7701502f2)

For YBCO, oxygen content alone omits chain configuration. Liang combined oxygen control, ordered-chain preparations and room-temperature lattice measurements; $T_c$ was the midpoint of field-cooled magnetization in 1 Oe along the c axis. Fully oxygenated material's 85.3 K transition anchors the overdoped calibration through the empirical parabola. It is not a new direct measurement of $p$. Record thermal history, chain order, calibration assumptions and their uncertainty alongside oxygen stoichiometry. [R2](#ref-source-e24ef58054ac9b71)

In the P-substituted Ba122 author manuscript, composition was measured by energy-dispersive X-ray analysis. Figure 2 separates onset and zero-resistivity temperatures. Version 3 reports zero resistance from $x\geq0.20$ and a maximum transition around 31 K, while observing a narrow transition at $x=0.33$. Do not turn “isovalent” into proof of unchanged Fermi surfaces, scattering or carrier populations. Nor does a similar phase diagram make chemical substitution physically identical to applied pressure. [R4](#ref-source-b9e77d2ee024ff3b)

Li's nickelate study concerns 8–10 nm films on SrTiO3, capped and reduced from perovskite precursors. Its superconducting compositions lie inside $0.125<x<0.25$. Figure 2 defines two temperatures at 10% and 90% of each sample's resistance at 20 K; symbols average multiple samples and error bars show extrema. The comparison cuprate curve instead uses 50%. These are unequal criteria. The 20 K normalization is not a superconducting transition, and the sign-changing Hall response is interpreted through multiple bands. [R5](#ref-source-c636cc691b7ff013)

## Limits and disputes

Changing composition and introducing pair-breaking disorder can occur together. Sidis's original abstract reports different neutron-resonance responses to Ni and Zn impurities in YBCO. That comparison does not justify a universal impurity ranking across pairing states or substitution sites. Separate scattering, magnetic moments, charge redistribution, structural effects and phase purity before inferring the origin of a $T_c$ trend. Abstract-only evidence cannot support a detailed impurity-dose reconstruction. [R6](#ref-source-9fd0a423b4e4de89)

Version and specimen checks remain necessary. Ni's author manuscript mentions 0.078 in its specific-heat discussion while the phase-summary maximum uses 0.074; this page does not silently identify them as one specimen. Kasahara's earlier and later manuscripts differ in the composition range and reported zero-resistance boundary, and the later figure's color description conflicts with the displayed onset/zero labels. Use the labeled criteria, not color alone. Final journal comparisons and raw-data reproduction remain open. [R3](#ref-source-cf1286d7701502f2) [R4](#ref-source-b9e77d2ee024ff3b)

A dome does not establish a unique pairing mechanism, homogeneous microscopic coexistence or a universal optimal carrier concentration. The nickelate manuscript notes sample-to-sample resistivity variations associated with microstructure and does not establish the origin of its small dip within the dome. Preserve that uncertainty; numerical resemblance to a cuprate anomaly is not an ordering measurement. [R5](#ref-source-c636cc691b7ff013)

## Discovery implications

Store nominal and measured composition separately, including the chemical formula convention, analytical method, uncertainty and spatial variation. Link each transition to its specimen, phase, oxygen/reduction history, substrate or strain, measurement criterion and source version. Keep Hall-derived model parameters separate from valence arithmetic. Prioritize reproducible composition–structure–property relationships and independent magnetic or thermodynamic checks before ranking a tuning series. The calculations here verify notation and algebra, not an optimal synthesis or a carrier-density calibration.
