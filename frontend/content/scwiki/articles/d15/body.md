## Scope

Magnetic substitution can change carrier balance, crystal structure, potential scattering, spin-flip scattering and exchange fields at the same time. These effects are distinguishable experimental questions, not mutually exclusive channels. A falling superconducting transition temperature does not uniquely identify magnetic pair breaking, and the presence of a magnetic element does not establish magnetically mediated pairing. Compare actual composition, valence, phase purity and preparation before treating concentration as a single physical control. [R3](#ref-source-8d0e592da8ec9c7b) [R4](#ref-source-23c62b46f5681d83) [R5](#ref-source-9ff1d5edeb007a39) [R11](#ref-source-3504ee9027165d0a)

Dilute paramagnetic impurities, an ordered lattice of rare-earth moments and a concentrated magnetic film require different models. Independent magnetic ions can scatter Cooper pairs, while their ordering, screening and coupling to itinerant states change the relevant timescales and fields. A material that contains both magnetic and superconducting signals may also be spatially inhomogeneous; their coexistence must be tested at the relevant volume and length scale. ## Mathematical assumptions

For dilute, uncorrelated weak magnetic scattering in an isotropic singlet host, define a pair-breaking energy $\Gamma$ through $\ln(T_{c0}/T_c)=\psi[1/2+\Gamma/(2\pi k_BT_c)]-\psi(1/2)$. Here $T_{c0}$ is the corresponding clean-host transition, not necessarily the transition of the unsubstituted parent if substitution also changes its bands or interaction. With $g=\Gamma/(k_BT_{c0})$ and $t=T_c/T_{c0}$, this convention gives $dt/dg=-\pi/4$ initially and $g_c=\pi e^{-\gamma_E}/2\simeq0.882$. Scattering-rate conventions require explicit factors of $\hbar$; neither atomic fraction nor residual resistivity can be inserted directly as $\Gamma$. The equation convention has a scoped review-paper check, while the original AG full text remains unaudited. [R1](#ref-source-daf8f301de81a726) [R2](#ref-source-3a528aeee1b7cf04) [R4](#ref-source-23c62b46f5681d83)

An independent inversion illustrates the ambiguity. In units with $k_B=1$, prescribe an observed curve $T_c=1-0.6c$, where $c$ is a synthetic dimensionless control. Both a fixed clean-host scale $T_{c0}=1$ and a changing scale $T_{c0}=1-0.2c$ admit different positive $\Gamma(c)$ values that reproduce the same transitions. Five checked points demonstrate non-uniqueness; they are not alloy fits or concentration calibrations. Additional composition, band, magnetic and scattering measurements are needed to choose between such explanations. [R2](#ref-source-3a528aeee1b7cf04) [R3](#ref-source-8d0e592da8ec9c7b) [R4](#ref-source-23c62b46f5681d83)

For a free Eu2+ ion with $S=7/2$ and $g=2$, the saturated moment is $gS\mu_B=7\mu_B$, whereas the Curie effective moment is $g\sqrt{S(S+1)}\mu_B\simeq7.94\mu_B$. Neither specifies the sign or strength of the exchange field acting on conduction electrons. In a collinear compensation illustration, $B_{\mathrm{eff}}=B_{\mathrm{ext}}+B_{\mathrm{ex}}$ and opposite signed contributions can reduce Zeeman splitting. This does not eliminate the orbital action of the applied field. Local-moment size, exchange energy and magnetic induction have different units and roles. ## Experimental criteria

Sugawara and Eguchi measured transition temperatures and critical fields magnetically in dilute La–Ce and La–Gd alloys. Their original abstract reports deviations from the AG Born-approximation result that depend on the exchange sign, interpreting them as evidence of Kondo effects. It also describes behavior characteristic of inhomogeneous type-II superconductors. This is a historical test of the theory's limits, not permission to assign one universal critical impurity fraction or transition-temperature slope to magnetic substitutions. [R5](#ref-source-9ff1d5edeb007a39)

Anderson's robustness argument for nonmagnetic disorder applies when appropriate time-reversed states, an essentially unchanged pairing interaction and density of states, and extended electronic states remain available. It is not immunity to arbitrary chemical substitution. Prozorov's controlled electron-irradiation study of a ruthenium-substituted iron arsenide illustrates why intra- and interband scattering, annealing and sample continuity matter. Nonmagnetic disorder can suppress sign-changing pairing, so comparing magnetic and nonmagnetic substitutions alone does not supply an independent singlet verdict. [R3](#ref-source-8d0e592da8ec9c7b) [R4](#ref-source-23c62b46f5681d83)

Cava established superconductivity in the LnNi2B2C family, including magnetic rare-earth members. Cho's cited paper then studied single-crystal ErNi2B2C specifically: transport and magnetization show a superconducting transition around 10.5 K, while the magnetic transition lies near 6 K. Its heat-capacity estimate is $5.85\pm0.15$ K. These are distinct probe-specific scales. The paper is not a systematic survey of all rare-earth ions, and changing the rare earth also changes crystal fields, structure and exchange. No universal free-ion-moment ranking follows. [R6](#ref-source-4a5af9d54dea74ba) [R7](#ref-source-e4fdbfe063c34217)

Yaron's small-angle neutron study provides a more direct coupling test. On a boron-11-enriched ErNi2B2C crystal, superconducting vortex diffraction and magnetic diffraction establish their relative orientations. Figure 1's vortex pattern is measured at 1.6 K and 4 kOe with field near the c axis; the magnetic comparison uses zero field and a different neutron wavelength. The measured vortex lines rotate away from the applied field as temperature falls, and their rocking curves broaden. These observations support microscopic coexistence through interaction between magnetic order and the vortex structure, rather than merely coincident bulk anomalies. [R8](#ref-source-6666556b639390d7)

The geometry matters: the applied field in that experiment was already tilted about 1.6 degrees from the c axis. The additional vortex rotation begins around 4 K and can approach one degree, whereas antiferromagnetic ordering occurs near 6 K. These temperatures should not be equated. The authors also consider long-distance orientation variation as an alternative contribution to rocking-curve broadening; width alone is not a unique measure of microscopic pinning or a demonstration of triplet pairing. [R8](#ref-source-6666556b639390d7)

Meul's Eu–Sn molybdenum chalcogenide work reports magnetic-field-induced superconductivity interpreted through exchange compensation. The checked original abstract supports that historical connection, but does not establish every numerical endpoint or the precise alloy composition used in earlier summaries. A compensation claim in another material needs its own signed exchange estimate, local-moment response, angle dependence and orbital constraints. Large upper critical fields or field-enhanced resistance drops alone are insufficient. [R9](#ref-source-849f7f4f675464de) [R10](#ref-source-d564bee026e788c7)

Yang's 2026 Sm/Ca/Eu infinite-layer nickelate study involves rare-earth substitution, fixed nominal Ca content and film reduction. In the checked later author version, SCE0.34 regains zero resistance at 2 K beyond 6.23 T; SCE0.32 does so at 3 K by 13.04 T. The inherited SCE0.36 / 0.3 K / 45 T tuple is not verified by the selected later-author-version panels: Extended Data Figure 9 labels SCE0.36 at 4.2 K and SCE0.40 at 0.3 K. It is withheld pending version and sample reconciliation. These are different samples and measurement temperatures, not a single transition curve. Compensation-like explanations remain under discussion; neither established uniform ferromagnetism nor triplet pairing follows from the reported reentrance alone. [R11](#ref-source-3504ee9027165d0a)

## Limits and disputes

The same substitution series may move a clean-host pairing scale, alter scattering and create an exchange contribution. An AG-looking fit cannot separate these without additional constraints. Conversely, a departure from simple AG behavior does not automatically prove unconventional pairing or exchange compensation. Complete scientific acceptance remains open: original AG and several classic full texts, final-version comparisons, calibration details and raw-data reanalysis remain unfinished. The numerical exercises check restricted inference and units, not a complete magnetic-alloy theory. ## Discovery implications

Track chemical composition and valence alongside diffraction, magnetic response, transport and thermodynamics. Preserve the distinction between dilute moments, ordered sublattices and possible glassy or inhomogeneous behavior. Keep onset, midpoint, measured-zero, ordering and measurement temperatures separate. For compensation, constrain the electronic exchange field and its uncertainty independently; for coexistence, seek a probe of coupling or spatial overlap. Use those measurements to distinguish explanations before extrapolating a concentration trend to a new material.
