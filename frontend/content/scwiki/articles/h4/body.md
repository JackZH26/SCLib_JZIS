## Scope

A charge-density wave (CDW) modulates the electronic density at a finite wave vector, often together with atomic displacements. Cuprates and kagome compounds exhibit several distinct charge-order patterns. Their coexistence or competition with superconductivity depends on composition, temperature, field, pressure and the probe's spatial and temporal resolution. Neither a density modulation nor a nearby superconducting dome identifies the pairing interaction. This page addresses observations and their interpretation. Pair-density waves instead modulate the superconducting pair field; spin-density waves modulate magnetization. Charge order, structural chirality, broken time-reversal symmetry and superconducting chirality are different claims. The retained source chapter's citation of Stahl for the conjoined-CDW paper is corrected: that paper is by Li and colleagues; Stahl's temperature-dependent stacking study has a different DOI. ## Mathematical assumptions

A single-component modulation can be written as $\rho(\mathbf r)=\rho_0+\operatorname{Re}[\rho_{\mathbf Q}e^{i\mathbf Q\cdot\mathbf r}]$. Peak position identifies periodicity, while width constrains correlations under a stated line-shape model. For the illustrative lattice correlation $C(n)=r^{|n|}$, with $r=e^{-1/\xi}$ and $\xi$ in lattice spacings, the Fourier sum is $S(q)=(1-r^2)/(1+r^2-2r\cos q)$. Here $q$ is dimensionless radians per spacing, measured from the ordering wave vector. Finite $\xi$ produces a finite-width peak; observing that peak does not establish infinite-range order. This is an independent correlation model, not a fit to the cited specimens. [R4](#ref-source-d9477b85392d03f9) [R5](#ref-source-b89f6bcf54acb4d8)

Correlation lengths also depend on resolution and conventions. If both intrinsic and instrumental profiles are Gaussian, $\sigma_{\rm obs}^2=\sigma_{\rm int}^2+\sigma_R^2$. The convention $\xi=1/\sigma_{\rm int}$ then requires momentum widths in inverse-length units. Reciprocal-lattice widths need the appropriate lattice conversion. Uncorrected $1/\sigma_{\rm obs}$ is a lower bound within this model. Gaussian standard deviations, Lorentzian half-widths and full widths are not interchangeable; a resolution-limited peak yields a bound rather than a precisely measured divergence. [R4](#ref-source-d9477b85392d03f9) [R5](#ref-source-b89f6bcf54acb4d8)

A phenomenological free energy $F=a_s|\Delta|^2+b_s|\Delta|^4+a_c|\rho_{\mathbf Q}|^2+b_c|\rho_{\mathbf Q}|^4+g|\Delta|^2|\rho_{\mathbf Q}|^2$ illustrates static competition when $g>0$. With positive quartic coefficients, a stable interior coexistence minimum additionally requires $4b_sb_c-g^2>0$ and positive equilibrium squared amplitudes. Competition alone neither guarantees coexistence nor determines the frequency-dependent pairing kernel. Charge fluctuations could contribute to an effective interaction in a specified microscopic model without making every observed CDW a pairing mechanism. ## Experimental criteria

Tranquada's historical neutron result concerns Nd-substituted La1.6−xNd0.4SrxCuO4 at $x=0.12$. Its static stripe evidence and suppressed superconductivity should not be assigned indiscriminately to every one-eighth-doped cuprate. Ghiringhelli's resonant X-ray study adds an element-sensitive charge response in underdoped YBCO. The selected author text uses 130 meV instrumental resolution and argues for a much smaller energy bound; that bound is not a separately resolved sharp collective mode. [R1](#ref-source-b33aab72ae697f3a) [R2](#ref-source-9ae65971bc3eb5b8)

Chang's 2012 experiment on detwinned ortho-VIII YBa2Cu3O6.67 finds charge-order intensity suppressed on entering the superconducting regime and enhanced by magnetic field below it. Those controlled changes support competition in this specimen. They do not isolate every field-induced change in vortices or electronic structure, and do not identify a unique microscopic interaction. [R3](#ref-source-c4a9d4e1b52d95bb)

Gerber's high-field diffraction shows additional interlayer-correlated order developing above about 15 T at 10 K. The author Figure 3 separates peaks near integer and half-integer out-of-plane coordinates and extracts Gaussian widths. Its reported lengths are explicitly uncorrected for resolution. Chang's 2016 study instead reports resolution-corrected widths: for YBCO6.67, the c-axis scale reaches about four lattice periods within the measured field range. Finite three-dimensional correlations must remain distinct from unlimited long-range coherence. These analyses cannot be combined by silently treating corrected and uncorrected widths as equivalent. [R4](#ref-source-d9477b85392d03f9) [R5](#ref-source-b89f6bcf54acb4d8)

Kagome studies probe different structures and surfaces. Liang's STM step-edge phase shift suggests $2\times2\times2$ order. Stahl's bulk diffraction reports a low-temperature $2\times2\times2$ structure changing to $2\times2\times4$ near 60 K on warming; its null response to fields up to 10 T does not rule out broken time-reversal symmetry. Li's separate CsV3Sb5 study resolves conjoined $2\times2\times1$ and $2\times2\times2$ charge-order responses, with distinct resonant behavior and a roughly 4 K onset separation at 1 GPa. That separation concerns charge order, not two superconducting transitions. [R6](#ref-source-7746049011a0e640) [R7](#ref-source-d773a884dacbd7c9) [R8](#ref-source-80eda571fc6d710c)

## Limits and disputes

A scattering superlattice peak needs electronic, structural and instrumental interpretation; it is not by itself proof of an exclusively electronic instability. STM conductance modulations must be compared across bias, surface termination and bulk probes. Li's surface modulation and bulk-null comparison illustrates why a surface pattern cannot automatically be promoted to a universal bulk phase. The present review does not independently reproduce those sensitivity limits or resolve every reported stacking structure. [R6](#ref-source-7746049011a0e640) [R8](#ref-source-80eda571fc6d710c)

Mielke's accepted-manuscript abstract reports enhanced internal-field widths in KV3Sb5 below charge ordering, interpreted as time-reversal symmetry breaking. This is a normal-state charge-order claim, not a direct determination of chiral superconducting pairing. Chen's spatially modulated tunnelling signatures motivate a kagome PDW interpretation, but interpretation of the pair field requires additional evidence beyond ordinary charge contrast. Material identity, probe coupling and the superconducting transition must remain explicit. [R9](#ref-source-484da7abcc165cd3) [R10](#ref-source-057c43c86174f961)

Complete scientific acceptance remains open. Selected methods and figures, historical abstracts and independent line-shape checks do not replace full source-version comparisons, raw diffraction reproduction, all supplementary calibrations or competing-structure assessment. A Hall anomaly, a Fermi-surface pocket or a charge onset below a pseudogap scale cannot alone specify which charge structure causes reconstruction or whether it mediates pairing. ## Discovery implications

Preserve the measured wave vector and reciprocal units, sample composition, oxygen order, field direction, pressure medium, line shape and resolution treatment. Compare competing-order amplitudes on characterized specimens and distinguish a static order parameter from dynamic spectral weight. Useful predictions should connect a particular charge structure to a testable electronic reconstruction and superconducting response, including results that could falsify the proposed connection.
