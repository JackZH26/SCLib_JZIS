## Scope

Mottness describes how electronic interactions constrain charge motion near integer filling and influence the states reached by doping or changing bandwidth. It is a useful starting point for some superconductors, not a unique pairing interaction. Anderson's RVB proposal is one historical route from a correlated parent; competing orders and several pairing descriptions remain part of the cuprate problem. [R2](#ref-source-5a6026d6455bf7a0) [R4](#ref-source-6c1991333d4a6fed)

Distinguish Mott-Hubbard and charge-transfer descriptions. In a localized configuration picture, the former emphasizes the cost of redistributing electrons between metal sites; the latter can involve a lower-energy ligand-to-metal excitation. Hybridization and bandwidth complicate that comparison. The Zaanen–Sawatzky–Allen paper provides the classification anchor, but its full text has not been checked here. The selected Lee–Nagaosa–Wen manuscript explicitly retains oxygen holes in cuprates before discussing a restricted low-energy reduction. A fitted single-band interaction is not automatically the bare metal-orbital Coulomb energy. [R1](#ref-source-38cb545895463a79) [R3](#ref-source-2f6a1c773323226a)

## Mathematical assumptions

An independent Hubbard-dimer illustration separates charge and spin scales. Take two sites, two spin states per site, hopping $t>0$, repulsion $U\geq0$, and no ligand orbital or reservoir. With $E_N$ the lowest energy at fixed particle number, define the finite-system charge addition/removal gap $\Delta_c=E_3+E_1-2E_2$. Direct diagonalization gives

$$\Delta_c=\sqrt{U^2+16t^2}-2t,\qquad \Delta_s=\frac{\sqrt{U^2+16t^2}-U}{2}.$$

Here $\Delta_s$ is the two-electron singlet–triplet gap. Eight independent spectra verify these expressions and the total double occupancy obtained from $\partial E_2/\partial U$. At $U=0$, the dimer already has a charge gap $2t$ from its discrete bonding levels. A finite-cluster charge gap therefore does not establish a bulk Mott transition. For $U\gg t$, the spin scale approaches $4t^2/U$ while the charge cost increases. Neither is a superconducting gap or a predicted transition temperature. The cited review supplies the Hubbard-model context; this calculation is an independent illustration. [R3](#ref-source-2f6a1c773323226a)

For a second atomic illustration, prescribe a spin-balanced ensemble with empty-site probability $x$, each singly occupied state of probability $(1-x)/2$, and no double occupancy. Counting electron creation and removal matrix elements gives, after summing both spins,

$$W_{\mathrm{remove}}=1-x,\qquad W_{\mathrm{add,low}}=2x,\qquad W_{\mathrm{add,upper}}=1-x.$$

The total spectral weight is two; the lower-energy removal-plus-addition weight is $1+x$, rather than a fixed rigid band with only its occupation changed. Five enumerated cases check the sum rule. This is a zero-hopping state-counting result with a specified ensemble, not a quantitative X-ray absorption spectrum. Finite hopping, ligand character and a core hole alter the interpretation of a measured spectrum. [R3](#ref-source-2f6a1c773323226a) [R8](#ref-source-1756566a3dd37c2c)

Configuration mixing also prevents an orbital label from being an integer occupancy. For the illustrative matrix $H=\begin{pmatrix}0&V\\V&\Delta_{\mathrm{CT}}\end{pmatrix}$ in two configuration states, the lower eigenstate has second-state weight $[1-\Delta_{\mathrm{CT}}/\sqrt{\Delta_{\mathrm{CT}}^2+4V^2}]/2$. Eight eigenvector and energy-derivative comparisons verify this result. The symbol $\Delta_{\mathrm{CT}}$ denotes a configuration-energy difference in this toy model; it is not a measured gap. This two-state calculation omits the lattice, multiplets and screening and cannot determine a material's ZSA phase boundary. [R1](#ref-source-38cb545895463a79) [R3](#ref-source-2f6a1c773323226a)

## Experimental criteria

Chen and colleagues measured the oxygen K edge of La2-xSrxCuO4+δ using bulk-sensitive fluorescence yield. Their two pre-edge features evolve with Sr concentration; the reported modeling includes local interactions and core-hole excitonic correlations. This supports a doped charge-transfer interpretation without making absorption intensity a direct ground-state density of states or proving a pairing glue. The present check is abstract-scoped. [R8](#ref-source-1756566a3dd37c2c)

Lefebvre and colleagues' kappa-Cl organic-salt study connects a pressure-driven Mott boundary with nearby antiferromagnetism and superconductivity. Its helium-pressure protocol includes 0.5 T NMR and AC susceptibility without a static field. Those are different measurement conditions, and the selected manuscript leaves macroscopic versus mesoscopic coexistence unresolved. No fixed pressure or transition temperature is transferred to other salts. [R5](#ref-source-2cc5358d54a65217)

Sun and colleagues' 2023 bilayer-nickelate report must retain sample and probe distinctions: four-probe resistance, KBr versus no pressure medium, nonzero residual resistance, and separately measured magnetic response. The published methods use room-temperature ruby calibration. Pressure changes structure as well as electronic parameters; the reported signatures do not identify Mottness as the pairing cause. [R7](#ref-source-df0c15d62982f853)

## Limits and disputes

CeCu2Si2 supplies a heavy-fermion comparison, not a copy of a one-band copper-oxide model. Its original discovery supports superconductivity with strongly enhanced electronic response; that report does not settle a unique gap or pairing glue. Comparisons involving localized f states and itinerant coherence require a material-specific Kondo description. Effective mass alone is not a measurement of a Mott charge gap. [R6](#ref-source-0ebda9b697c1f7fd)

Mottness is neither sufficient nor a universal prerequisite for superconductivity. MgB2 provides a multiband electron–phonon calculation, and sulfur-hydride experiments provide a distinct high-pressure setting. These examples prevent treating a doped Mott parent as a necessary route to a high transition scale. The inherited MgB2 DOI has been corrected to **nature00898**; the old **nature00992** points to an unrelated biological channel paper. [R9](#ref-source-7c4eac077ae5e221) [R10](#ref-source-8b901b9c6008c434)

## Discovery implications

State the orbital basis, interaction convention and screening method before comparing $U/t$ or charge-transfer energies. Combine transport with spectroscopic and structural measurements, preserving pressure media, oxygen content and temperature criteria. Treat localization, pseudogap, magnetic exchange, pairing and phase coherence as distinct questions. Full scientific acceptance, final-version comparisons and raw-data checks remain open; the finite models illustrate constraints rather than rank real materials.
