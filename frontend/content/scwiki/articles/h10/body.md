## Scope

A quantum spin liquid is a quantum magnetic state with correlations and excitations beyond a conventional ordered magnet. Absence of static magnetic order alone is not a definition that identifies it experimentally: a trivial singlet state, disorder or a finite measurement window can also suppress the expected signal. Candidate materials require positive evidence and tests of alternatives. Chiral spin liquids can break time-reversal symmetry, so preserving every conventional symmetry is not a universal requirement. [R1](#ref-source-779d3cdcbcd5a620) [R2](#ref-source-4619c72961e466c5) [R6](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b)

The connection to superconductivity is a research route, not an automatic consequence. Anderson's RVB proposal motivates doping a correlated singlet state, but undoped La2CuO4 has antiferromagnetic order. A spin-only Hamiltonian has no mobile electrical charge or superconducting stiffness. Emergent neutral Majorana fermions in such a model do not make its material a topological superconductor. A specific charge sector and gauge-invariant superconducting response must be established separately. ## Mathematical assumptions

An explicit counterexample clarifies the diagnostic problem. A product of disconnected spin singlets has $\langle\mathbf S_i\rangle=0$ and vanishing correlations between different dimers, yet is not thereby a long-range-entangled spin liquid. For two spin-one-half singlets, direct matrix evaluation gives $\langle\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2\rangle=-3/4$ and $\langle\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_3\rangle=0$. These finite-state checks disprove the inference from zero local moments alone; they do not classify an interacting bulk material. [R2](#ref-source-4619c72961e466c5) [R6](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b)

Use the honeycomb convention $H=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle_\alpha}J_\alpha\sigma_i^\alpha\sigma_j^\alpha$, with Pauli matrices $\sigma=2S$. A Hamiltonian written with spin operators requires couplings $K_\alpha=4J_\alpha$ for the same energy. The vortex-free zero-field matter spectrum has zeros when the three $|J_\alpha|$ satisfy triangle inequalities. Kitaev calls this the gapless B region, including isotropy; the dominant-coupling A regions are gapped. The source draft had these labels reversed. [R1](#ref-source-779d3cdcbcd5a620)

Near isotropy, a weak field with all three spin-axis components nonzero generates a time-reversal-breaking term scaling as $h_xh_yh_z/J^2$ at third order. This perturbative result is not exact solvability at arbitrary field. For an ideal equilibrated chiral Majorana edge above a gapped bulk, $\kappa_{xy}^{2D}/T=c_-\pi^2k_B^2/(3h)$ with $c_-=\pm1/2$. This is thermal conductance per layer, not three-dimensional conductivity or electrical Hall conductance. Independent checks verify the phase-boundary algebra, spin normalization and positive-energy thermal integral. [R1](#ref-source-779d3cdcbcd5a620)

For a gapped two-dimensional topologically ordered ground state and a sufficiently large smooth region, $S=\alpha L-\gamma+\cdots$ with $\gamma=\ln\mathcal D$ and $\mathcal D=\sqrt{\sum_a d_a^2}$. The Abelian Z2 example gives $\gamma=\ln2$. This expression is not a universal entropy formula for every gapless spin liquid, and a single entropy constant does not fully specify anyon statistics. Finite geometry, boundaries and correlation length matter before identifying a universal term. [R2](#ref-source-4619c72961e466c5)

## Experimental criteria

Shimizu's organic candidate kappa-(BEDT-TTF)2Cu2(CN)3 shows no proton-NMR signature of long-range magnetic order down to 32 mK. The single-crystal experiment applied 2.2 T perpendicular to the conducting planes; the susceptibility-derived exchange scale around 250 K came from a separate fit. Neither number is a superconducting transition temperature, and the experiment does not directly observe the zero-field, zero-temperature ground state. Kurosaki's pressure phase diagram separately connects the correlated insulator with metallic and superconducting regions; it does not identify a unique pairing mechanism. [R4](#ref-source-9d15cd9236072f5c) [R5](#ref-source-0443485ac20d3a8c)

Pustogow's 2023 strain-transport study instead favors a low-entropy, spin-gapped valence-bond-solid interpretation of the organic material's 6 K anomaly. Its hydrostatic-equivalent pressure is inferred from comparison with transport data, rather than directly applied hydrostatic pressure. The paper retains a gapped chiral-spin-liquid alternative and discusses mounting-related microcracks. Thus a revised interpretation must preserve both the measured anomaly and the remaining discrimination problem. [R6](#ref-source-5e0dcd80e8b64a1b)

Han's neutron study used deuterated herbertsmithite, ZnCu3(OD)6Cl2, at 1.6 K. Broad momentum-energy scattering supports the authors' fractionalization interpretation, but the measured low-energy signal can include interlayer Cu defects. The apparent absence of a gap down to the measured energy limit does not establish a universally gapless intrinsic kagome state. A later primary study explicitly analyzes correlated impurity contributions. Preserve isotope, background subtraction, resolution and momentum window before comparing these results with a theoretical continuum. [R7](#ref-source-43fba6076983bec0) [R8](#ref-source-7a6863ba09a1c037)

In alpha-RuCl3, zero-field magnetic order and candidate field-induced behavior must be separated. Xing's 2025 ultraclean-crystal study reports a narrower near-half-quantized thermal Hall plateau and weakened oscillation-like features. It discusses sample-dependent magnetic boundaries, possible stacking faults and phonon-edge coupling. The 5.0 K Hall measurement used heat current along b and field along a; the comparison crystal was measured at 4.3 K. These are not identical conditions or a universal material threshold. [R9](#ref-source-f089ec733f27462c)

Shragai's 2026 ultrasound study measures acoustic Faraday rotation and infers a phonon Hall-viscosity contribution. Its conversion to thermal transport requires assumptions about frequency, phonon branches and viscosity components; the directly measured single-component estimate does not independently measure every thermal carrier. One displayed comparison also combines 2 K, 55-degree-tilted ultrasound with 4.9 K, 45-degree-tilted thermal data. This supplies substantive phonon evidence without making every field geometry or claimed quantized plateau equivalent. [R10](#ref-source-4a3e383e4c71604e)

## Limits and disputes

A continuum, a low-temperature heat-capacity term, absent NMR splitting and a thermal Hall signal test different observables. Their interpretation depends on defects, phonons, magnetic order, probe time scales and sample history. Do not turn one positive signal into a sufficient spin-liquid criterion, or one disputed material interpretation into a claim about every possible quantum spin liquid. Broad statements that all materials are settled or that none can ever be established would exceed this review. ## Discovery implications

Track the insulating magnetic state, tuning parameter and superconducting response independently. For a pressure or doping trajectory, distinguish structural changes and disorder from changed bandwidth or filling. Compare several probes on characterized samples and ask what would exclude a trivial singlet, ordered or disordered alternative. A successful spin-liquid model provides testable mechanisms and excitations; establishing superconductivity additionally requires mobile charge, phase coherence and the corresponding experimental criteria.
