## Scope

Iron-based superconductors include Fe-pnictogen and Fe-chalcogen layers in several structural families. Their low-energy electronic structure commonly involves several Fe 3d orbitals. Many parent compounds are metallic with coupled magnetic, structural or nematic order; this is a different starting point from a simple single-band cuprate analogy. Kamihara and colleagues' F-doped 1111 material established the early high-temperature platform. [R1](#ref-source-62a72493865c59d7)

Distinguish 1111, 122, 111 and 11 materials, and treat a single-layer interface separately from a bulk crystal. The K-doped 122 superconductor reported by Rotter and colleagues is not the undoped BaFe2As2 parent. The discovery of superconductivity in FeSe likewise does not imply that its excess-Fe content or electronic structure can be ignored. [R2](#ref-source-1a3690e5f1d38f3e) [R3](#ref-source-205024adeb431322)

## Mathematical assumptions

A multiband description uses gaps $\Delta_i(\mathbf k)$ on a measured set of bands or orbitals. A candidate s± solution has a relative sign between appropriate Fermi-surface sheets. It is a pairing-model result, not a consequence of the material containing iron. Changes in Fermi surfaces, shallow bands or orbital weights can change which candidate is favored. [R6](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c)

Doping notation also needs care. The numerical coefficient $x$ in $\mathrm{BaFe}_{2-x}\mathrm{Co}_x\mathrm{As}_2$ differs from the fraction $x$ in $\mathrm{Ba}(\mathrm{Fe}_{1-x}\mathrm{Co}_x)_2\mathrm{As}_2$. Comparing “optimal x” values without converting their definitions can manufacture a disagreement. This pilot avoids a universal optimum or a universal orbital-coupling table.

## Experimental criteria

Report structural family, composition, sample form, preparation and pressure with each superconducting observation. Separate a parent magnetic or structural transition from a superconducting transition in a substituted compound. State whether a temperature is an onset, zero resistance, bulk response or spectroscopic gap scale. Unknown criteria remain unknown in the structured records.

The Fe1.01Se pressure result is a concrete condition check: the cited report associates 36.7 K with 8.9 GPa, not an unqualified 1-2 GPa result. That value cannot be copied into an ambient-pressure materials entry. Formula and pressure accompany it in the HTML and API record. [R4](#ref-source-a631bcd83d23567b)

## Evidence for gap structure

The superconductivity-induced neutron response of Ba0.6K0.4Fe2As2 constrains sign-changing pairing models. It does not directly determine the gaps of all 1111, 122, FeSe or interface systems. A synthesis should state the measured wave vector, normal-state spectrum and candidate coherence-factor interpretation. [R5](#ref-source-716c338ddfa726d3) [R6](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c)

For an interface, replica bands or a high ARPES gap-closing scale must retain their spectroscopic labels. A reconstructed single-layer Fermi surface may differ substantially from the canonical hole-electron-pocket model. This is why the interface article and the multiband article are separate from a bulk-family temperature comparison. [R6](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c)

## Limits and disputes

Several orbitals and strong correlations provide useful organizing language, but they do not establish a universal pairing mechanism. Hund-related mass renormalization is not automatically a larger pairing eigenvalue. FeSe, strongly doped endpoints and interface systems are especially important tests of simple family-wide statements. A strong evidence chain for a candidate gap is different from a unique microscopic verdict. [R6](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c)

## Discovery implications

Use controlled changes in structure, composition, strain and disorder to test a specific model. Preserve the distinction between modifying a Fermi surface and identifying the boson that pairs electrons. The most useful database entry is a condition-rich measurement linked to its original compound, not one universal iron-based critical temperature.
