## Scope

Heavy-fermion superconductors pair electrons in a strongly renormalized metallic state. Their large low-temperature electronic heat capacity and magnetic response reflect interactions involving partially filled f shells, not unusually heavy atoms moving as Cooper pairs. CeCu₂Si₂ established this class in 1979; the discovery paper reported a transition near 0.5 K. Different compositions and probes need not reproduce that exact number. The family is a laboratory for competing correlations rather than a single pairing mechanism. [R1](#ref-source-0ebda9b697c1f7fd) [R5](#ref-source-5962cf8038597944)

Kondo screening, conduction-electron-mediated magnetic interactions, hybridization and valence changes provide useful organizing ideas. Doniach's competition between magnetic order and a compensated state is a model framework. Proximity to a magnetic boundary does not by itself identify the pairing interaction or predict a superconducting transition. Uranium compounds also require attention to itinerant 5f character and strong spin–orbit coupling; a label borrowed from a cerium compound is not a complete microscopic description. [R2](#ref-source-1bdac37fd450eb32) [R6](#ref-source-e7c77a3327daf5c6) [R9](#ref-source-15aec45a868e9fb9)

## Mathematical assumptions

In a Fermi-liquid regime, the electronic coefficient $\gamma=\lim_{T\to0}C_e/T$ is proportional to the renormalized density of states. A finite-temperature value of total $C/T$ is not automatically that limit. Petrovic's CeCoIn₅ report gives about 290 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² above 2.5 K but a much larger low-temperature normal-state value under field. Thus its apparent $\Delta C/(\gamma T_c)\approx4.5$ cannot be converted mechanically into a coupling strength using a temperature-independent normal-state baseline. [R5](#ref-source-5962cf8038597944)

Kittaka's CeCu₂Si₂ analysis subtracts a nuclear term. With numerical field $h$ in tesla and temperature $t$ in kelvin, its empirical expression is $C_n/T=(7.4h^2+0.1)t^{-3}\times10^{-6}$ J mol⁻¹ K⁻². At 60 mK it gives approximately 0.00046, 0.111 and 0.309 J mol⁻¹ K⁻² for 0, 1.8 and 3 T. These recalculations illustrate the size of the correction; they are not a new fit or evidence that any arbitrary subtraction is acceptable. [R3](#ref-source-2764bce38246bfae)

Pang's simplified band-mixing model uses $|\Delta(\varphi)|=\sqrt{a^2\cos^2(2\varphi)+b^2\sin^2(2\varphi)}$ at zero temperature. For nonzero amplitudes on the assumed cylinder, the minimum is $\min(|a|,|b|)$; exchanging them preserves angular averages. The reported S-type fit uses $a=2.5k_BT_c$ and $b=0.58k_BT_c$. This magnitude does not specify the full band matrix or its time-reversal properties. A scalar chiral gap can have the same magnitude; that algebraic coincidence does not equate the two physical states. [R4](#ref-source-e721bddb9ef11661)

## Experimental criteria

The CeCu₂Si₂ specific-heat study used a 13.8 mg S-type crystal, measurements down to 40 mK and a nuclear correction constrained by high-field normal-state data. It reported low-energy behavior consistent with full gaps and no detected in-plane angular oscillation within stated sensitivity. The result constrains quasiparticles weighted strongly by thermodynamics; it is not a universal exclusion of every possible small-band node. The author manuscript's February 2014 version must not be dated from its later PDF generation stamp. [R3](#ref-source-2764bce38246bfae)

Pang measured penetration-depth shifts down to about 40 mK using a tunnel-diode oscillator. Keep each reported temperature tied to its sample and criterion:

| Sample | Criterion in Pang | Approximate temperature |
|---|---|---|
| S-type | Resistive onset | 0.65 K |
| S-type | Zero resistivity | 0.60 K |
| S-type | Superconducting specific-heat anomaly | 0.64 K |
| S-type | Penetration-depth onset | 0.62 K |
| A/S-type | Resistive onset | 0.62 K |
| A/S-type | Superconducting specific-heat anomaly | 0.53 K |
| A/S-type | Antiferromagnetic specific-heat anomaly | 0.70 K; not superconducting Tc |

Low-temperature exponential fits extend to 0.12 K. Finite-window power-law fits are also reported; their exponents increase as the upper fitting temperature decreases. This supports a low-temperature full gap without uniquely selecting the pairing state. Superfluid-density conversion assumes a zero-temperature penetration depth of 2000 Å, and both two-gap s-wave and d+d fits describe it. The paper's sign-change argument additionally invokes earlier neutron and NQR evidence; it is not a direct phase measurement by the oscillator. Its later heat-capacity fit uses data digitized from Kittaka. Raw-data refitting and the complete supplement remain outside this scoped review. [R4](#ref-source-e721bddb9ef11661)

For CeCoIn₅, the discovery study combined zero resistance, magnetic shielding and a heat-capacity anomaly near 2.3 K. It also found substantially different upper critical fields parallel and perpendicular to the c axis. Later neutron work found magnetic order that disappears with superconductivity at the high-field boundary. A coupled magnetic Q phase therefore cannot be described as a clean demonstration of a standalone textbook FFLO state. [R5](#ref-source-5962cf8038597944) [R7](#ref-source-fda01ade7737b638)

## Limits and disputes

CeCu₂Si₂ should not be assigned an unqualified textbook nodal d-wave label. Full-gap thermodynamics, sign-sensitive interpretations and band structure must be reconciled. Its pressure response adds another question: Holmes measured resistivity and ac heat capacity above 6 GPa in helium and interpreted anomalies near 4.5 GPa through valence fluctuations. That interpretation is a proposed mechanism, not a direct measurement of a unique pairing interaction. [R3](#ref-source-2764bce38246bfae) [R4](#ref-source-e721bddb9ef11661) [R6](#ref-source-e7c77a3327daf5c6)

UPt₃ has multiple superconducting phases and Kerr evidence constraining time-reversal symmetry. Heating, field training and the phase being probed matter. This does not uniquely select f-wave symmetry or establish a topological device. URhGe and UCoGe provide coexistence evidence for ferromagnetism and superconductivity, but their magnetic and superconducting transition temperatures are distinct. Their respective field responses cannot be exchanged between compounds. [R8](#ref-source-d3edee404071ac81) [R9](#ref-source-15aec45a868e9fb9) [R10](#ref-source-a4695ba5bfa789f3)

UTe₂ requires particularly careful qualifiers. Antiferromagnetic neutron fluctuations, transverse high-field fluctuations and sample-dependent Kerr results probe different conditions. The 2023 Kerr study found no spontaneous signal in the tested single-transition samples; it does not negate every other superconducting phase. No single large upper critical field, fluctuation spectrum or null Kerr observation determines spin structure and topology together. [R11](#ref-source-df199ac9453d6985) [R13](#ref-source-15cfc4a2f9587af0) [R14](#ref-source-dd4e897d833ce69c)

## Discovery implications

The 2025 UTe₂ halo study mapped transport boundaries with pulsed fields, generally near 0.63 K, using resistance and contactless proximity-detector measurements. Tilting away from the b axis revealed an extended high-field region. The experiment reached approximately 73 T; extrapolated persistence beyond that field is not a measured upper boundary. Its multicomponent triplet interpretation remains a model conclusion. Supplementary sample details, heating corrections and the deposited raw data have not been independently reanalyzed here. [R12](#ref-source-0c4d7cebb12a0d5b)

The 2026 transverse-fluctuation study measured the normal state at 4 K; superconducting phase boundaries at 300 mK were comparisons with earlier work. Neither temperature is a newly measured transition temperature in that experiment. Discovery records should retain the sample, pressure, field direction, probe and criterion, then compare mechanisms across compatible conditions. The goal is to identify informative constraints, not rank unrelated temperatures or convert candidate topology into an established engineering capability. [R13](#ref-source-15cfc4a2f9587af0)
