## Scope

A Planckian-scale claim compares a defined relaxation rate with $k_BT/\hbar$. It must identify the observable, residual subtraction and uncertainty. Charge transport, optical response, single-particle decay and quantum scrambling need not share one lifetime. An approximate coefficient near unity is an empirical observation within a model, not a universal theorem obtained from a straight resistivity curve. [R1](#ref-source-67da95cac5481029)

The evidence has several levels: measured conductivity, an extracted rate, a cross-material scaling relation, and a proposed bound. Moving between them adds assumptions. Bruin's transport comparison, Legros's cuprate analysis, angular magnetoresistance and frequency-resolved film measurements test different parts of this chain. A useful comparison retains each experiment's conditions instead of pooling every quoted coefficient. ## Mathematical assumptions

Write $\gamma_{\mathrm{tr}}=\gamma_0+\alpha_{\mathrm{inel}}k_BT/\hbar$. Then $\alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}=\hbar\gamma_{\mathrm{tr}}/(k_BT)=\alpha_{\mathrm{inel}}+\hbar\gamma_0/(k_BT)$. A constant residual rate makes the total ratio temperature dependent even when the inelastic slope is fixed. Twenty-seven independent synthetic parameter checks verify this algebra; they do not justify additive scattering in a material. [R1](#ref-source-67da95cac5481029)

With temperature-independent density and mass in a single-band Drude model, $\rho=m^\ast\gamma_{\mathrm{tr}}/(ne^2)$ gives $\alpha_{\mathrm{inel}}=(\hbar ne^2/k_Bm^\ast)d\rho/dT$. A two-dimensional, spin-degenerate parabolic band instead uses sheet slope $A_1^\square$, areal density $n_{2D}$ and $T_F=\pi\hbar^2n_{2D}/(m^\ast k_B)$, yielding $A_1^\square T_F=\alpha_{\mathrm{inel}}h/(2e^2)$. The geometric conversion and mass estimate are essential inputs, not outputs of a resistivity slope. Noncircular or multiple Fermi surfaces require additional analysis. [R3](#ref-source-c1ae7869b672a8e8)

An independent angular-scattering illustration makes the lifetime distinction explicit. On a circular Fermi line, define an elastic event kernel $W(\theta)=\gamma[1+a\cos\theta]/(2\pi)$ with $|a|\leq1$. The event rate is $\int_0^{2\pi}W\,d\theta=\gamma$, whereas current relaxation weights changes of direction: $\int_0^{2\pi}W(1-\cos\theta)\,d\theta=\gamma(1-a/2)$. Fifteen numerical quadratures verify both integrals. Forward-weighted events therefore relax current less efficiently in this specified model. The event rate is explicitly defined; it is not silently identified with a spectral half-width. This elastic Boltzmann example proves neither a quantum bound nor a superconducting mechanism. [R1](#ref-source-67da95cac5481029)

For a fitted Drude denominator $\Gamma-i\nu$, both variables use cycles per second. The corresponding decay rate is $\gamma=2\pi\Gamma$, so $\Gamma=\alpha k_BT/h$ and $\gamma=\alpha k_BT/\hbar$ have the same dimensionless coefficient after conversion. Mixing the conventions without converting the fitted width changes the apparent coefficient by $2\pi$. Parallel channels add conductivities, further limiting inference from total resistance. [R4](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

## Experimental criteria

Bruin and colleagues report linear resistivity in Sr3Ru2O7 at 7.9 T and use independently characterized Fermi-surface inputs to estimate scattering. The retrieved opening page describes a 1.5–400 K transport study and a field-induced nematic region below 1.2 K near that field. Those boundaries matter: the available page does not justify extending the measured law through the intervening phase or verify every cross-material fit. The remaining article pages and supplementary tables are outside this check. [R2](#ref-source-e6d342432f3409ae)

Legros's author manuscript compares sheet-resistance slopes with Fermi-temperature estimates. Its electron-doped comparison gives $\alpha=1.0\pm0.3$; the hole-doped estimates require more approximate geometry and specific-heat masses, with temperature-dependent mass an explicit complication in Nd-LSCO. The displayed $\alpha=1$ prediction is a tested assumption, not an independent lifetime measurement. This article uses selected author-version passages; a complete comparison with the final publication remains open. [R3](#ref-source-c1ae7869b672a8e8)

Grissonnanche's final Nd-LSCO analysis separates an isotropic temperature-dependent component from anisotropic elastic scattering and reports $\alpha=1.2\pm0.4$. The band-energy scale is constrained by specific heat, and the quoted uncertainty includes fitting and sample-geometry contributions. Measurements at 45 T include superconducting angular sectors at the lower temperatures; those sectors are excluded. Neither a large applied field nor the isotropic inelastic term makes every datum normal-state or the total scattering isotropic. [R6](#ref-source-374c8bc13ced83e8)

Romero's author manuscript fits two FeTe1−xSex films over 20–40 K using a narrow Drude contribution plus a broad background. It reports cycle-frequency coefficients 3.02 for $x=0.45$ and 2.42 for $x=0.35$, with widths below 0.2 THz unresolved in its spectral window. These are author-version fit values, not newly reproduced final-paper estimates. The published abstract confirms a parallel-channel interpretation; complete version comparison and fitting covariance remain open. A linear narrow-channel width can coexist with nonlinear total resistivity. [R4](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

## Limits and disputes

The conditional chaos result $\lambda_L\leq2\pi k_BT/\hbar$ bounds exponential growth of a suitable regularized out-of-time-order correlator. Its argument requires thermal-strip analyticity and boundedness, controlled factorization errors and separated dissipation and scrambling scales. This is not a measured charge-transport rate; identifying the exponent with one requires another, model-specific argument. The theorem alone neither fixes a transport coefficient to one nor verifies a material's inferred mass. [R5](#ref-source-8339743a9f1c3239) [R1](#ref-source-67da95cac5481029)

Complete scientific acceptance remains open. Selected primary passages, figures and independent model checks do not constitute raw-data reproduction. Distinguish published results from the author's interpretation of universality; preserve unresolved frequency coverage, final-version and calibration limits. Spectral weight entering a condensate also does not identify the pairing mediator. [R2](#ref-source-e6d342432f3409ae) [R3](#ref-source-c1ae7869b672a8e8) [R4](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

## Discovery implications

Compare rates only after aligning channel definitions, sample conditions and frequency units. Test whether alternative band weights or a temperature-dependent mass can explain the same measured curve. Then seek independent optical or angle-dependent constraints. A connection between normal-state relaxation and superconductivity is a research hypothesis to test; faster scattering does not, by itself, predict a higher transition temperature.
