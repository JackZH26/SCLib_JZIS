## Scope

The label s± describes a superconducting gap whose sign differs between relevant Fermi-surface sheets. In common iron-based models the state belongs to the fully symmetric A1g representation, while different pockets carry opposite gap signs. A fully open gap therefore does not establish a sign-preserving state. The precise pocket and orbital assignments must come from the actual material rather than a generic two-pocket drawing. [R1](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c) [R2](#ref-source-f80a18149666bd31)

## Mathematical assumptions

A minimal illustration is

$
\Delta_h=+\Delta_1,\qquad
\Delta_e=-\Delta_2.
$

The labels $h$ and $e$ refer to hole and electron pockets in the model, not to a universal inventory present in every iron-based superconductor. Repulsive interband scattering can favor opposite signs in a linearized pairing equation. Mazin and colleagues and Kuroki and colleagues developed important iron-based proposals, but their predictions depend on the Fermi surface and interaction assumptions. [R1](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c) [R2](#ref-source-f80a18149666bd31)

An A1g gap can also be anisotropic and can develop accidental nodes. This possibility should be distinguished from symmetry-enforced nodes in another representation. Observing nodes alone cannot choose among every candidate, and no one compound determines the gap of an entire family. [R1](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c) [R2](#ref-source-f80a18149666bd31)

## Experimental criteria

A neutron resonance can be consistent with a sign-changing coherence factor, but its interpretation depends on the normal-state response and gap structure. Christianson and colleagues studied K-doped Ba0.6K0.4Fe2As2; the pinned chapter's Co-doped attribution is corrected here. Their result provides a material-specific constraint rather than a universal direct phase measurement. [R3](#ref-source-716c338ddfa726d3)

Quasiparticle interference depends on scattering channels, matrix elements, field conditions and a band model. Hanaguri and colleagues' Fe(Se,Te) study is not a measurement on pure bulk FeSe, and its evidence should not be paraphrased as directly photographing a negative gap sign. Complementary phase-sensitive junctions, controlled disorder studies and gap spectroscopy can narrow alternatives. [R4](#ref-source-3ee65ce8fc5080da)

## Corrected nodal example

Reid and colleagues' 2012 thermal-conductivity paper, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.087001, is about KFe2As2. The authors report residual heat transport consistent with nodes and argue for a d-wave interpretation. It is not a P-substituted Ba122 paper. The previous attribution is corrected in the source mapping and in the register for C2 and C10. No replacement P-Ba122 claim is silently inferred from this DOI. [R5](#ref-source-8a117f38b78526ff)

This correction matters physically: a source arguing for symmetry-imposed nodes cannot be repurposed as a demonstration of accidental nodes in another compound. The editorial record preserves the original path and hash so readers can identify what changed. [R5](#ref-source-8a117f38b78526ff)

## Limits and disputes

FeSe, shallow-band systems, strongly hole-doped endpoints and electron-only interfaces can require models different from the original hole-electron-pocket proposal. A candidate s± solution for a nickelate is a theoretical proposal until an appropriate experiment constrains it. Gap magnitude, gap sign, nodes and the microscopic pairing interaction are separate inference steps. [R1](#ref-source-7c2b55d128348e6c) [R2](#ref-source-f80a18149666bd31)

## Discovery implications

Specify which sheets are expected to change sign and what observation could reject that assignment. Use measured electronic structure, compare several independent probes and retain sample composition. For agent retrieval, quote the claim together with the named material and source; the sign structure is not an unconditional property of the phrase “iron-based superconductor.” [R3](#ref-source-716c338ddfa726d3) [R4](#ref-source-3ee65ce8fc5080da) [R5](#ref-source-8a117f38b78526ff)
