## Scope

A reported zero resistance is an operational result: the voltage is below the detection threshold for a specified current and contact geometry. The experiment establishes a bound on resistance rather than a mathematical proof that it is exactly zero. This article concerns electrical transport; magnetic expulsion and thermodynamic anomalies require separate measurements. The superconducting condensate provides the theoretical setting, but a microscopic model does not replace the experimental controls. [R1](#ref-source-ba10e800a1246bf6)

Record resistance onset, midpoint and zero separately. An onset can identify a small superconducting region, whereas a connected path may determine the zero-resistance result. Neither number alone measures the superconducting volume fraction. Filaments, an interface or a second phase can dominate a four-terminal path even when most of the specimen remains normal. This distinction is central when comparing bulk crystals, films and high-pressure samples. [R1](#ref-source-ba10e800a1246bf6)

## Mathematical assumptions

For a measured voltage resolution $V_{\mathrm{res}}$ and applied current $I$, an unresolved voltage implies the operational bound

$
R_{\mathrm{meas}} < \frac{V_{\mathrm{res}}}{|I|}.
$

This expression is a measurement definition, not a universal material law. It assumes a meaningful current path, stable contacts and a noise floor evaluated under the actual acquisition conditions. In a homogeneous specimen, converting resistance to resistivity further requires the sample dimensions. The ideal condensate response is a limiting statement about frequency, temperature, field and current; a finite experiment cannot take every limit simultaneously. [R1](#ref-source-ba10e800a1246bf6)

## Experimental criteria

Use four-terminal measurements and show the raw resistance-versus-temperature or voltage-versus-current data. Report excitation current or current density, voltage threshold, field direction, sweep direction, contact arrangement and sample history. Repeat measurements at different currents to distinguish a robust transition from heating, a contact short or an instrument floor. For a claim of bulk superconductivity, seek independent magnetic or thermodynamic evidence. These are editorial evidence requirements, not a claim that one universal protocol fits every apparatus.

In a two-dimensional system, a resistive tail can reflect phase fluctuations. BKT analysis requires more than a fit to one tail: non-linear current-voltage behavior, finite-size effects and stiffness measurements constrain the interpretation. Halperin-Nelson transport scaling applies in a specified two-dimensional fluctuation regime; it is not a general proof of pairing symmetry. [R2](#ref-source-6b3c996f39226a0a)

## Limits and disputes

Light-driven optical signatures must remain in a different evidence category from an equilibrium transport transition. Mitrano and colleagues reported a transient superconducting-like response in driven K3C60. The source chapter itself retains the ambiguity of assigning superconductivity from optics alone. Budden and colleagues investigated a longer-lived driven state with transport observations; those observations do not define an equilibrium zero-resistance critical temperature. This pilot does not promote either optical scale into a bulk temperature ranking. [R3](#ref-source-6ee3670220e24ff1) [R4](#ref-source-07be806c0002c690)

A persistent current can be complementary evidence, but its interpretation needs the loop geometry and decay time. A single low-resistance reading cannot supply those quantities. Likewise, magnetic-field suppression supports a superconducting interpretation without uniquely identifying the pairing interaction. [R1](#ref-source-ba10e800a1246bf6)

## Discovery implications

Treat zero resistance as one part of an evidence chain. A useful candidate report specifies a measurable threshold, demonstrates repeatability and connects transport to other probes. Preserve negative controls and the possibility of a minority superconducting path. This approach makes a discovery claim more testable while keeping unresolved volume fraction, phase identity and mechanism explicitly open.
