## Scope

A strange metal is identified through unusual normal-state behavior, often including resistivity approximately proportional to temperature after superconductivity is suppressed. It is an observational category, not a uniquely established microscopic phase or order parameter. The early Bi-based transport work and later overdoped-cuprate experiments concern particular specimens and temperature ranges. Their results do not provide a universal slope for all superconductors. Ordinary high-temperature electron–phonon transport can also be linear, so a room-temperature straight line alone is insufficient. [R1](#ref-source-a5d6480d08ee8555) [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225) [R4](#ref-source-374c8bc13ced83e8)

This article focuses on how observations constrain explanations. Linear resistivity, a transport relaxation rate near a thermal quantum scale, proximity to a quantum critical point and a high superconducting transition temperature are separate propositions. Each requires additional evidence. Even the identity of a relevant lifetime matters: a single-particle linewidth and a current-relaxation rate need not weight scattering processes in the same way. ## Mathematical assumptions

For a fitted normal-state curve, write $\rho(T)=\rho_0+AT+BT^2$. This is an empirical decomposition over a stated interval; its terms do not automatically identify independent microscopic mechanisms. Define a local exponent relative to a trial residual $\rho_r$ by $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=d\ln(\rho-\rho_r)/d\ln T$, wherever the logarithm is defined. Derivatives amplify noise and depend on the residual subtraction. An exponent should therefore accompany its temperature window, uncertainty and treatment of the superconducting region. [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225) [R3](#ref-source-c1ae7869b672a8e8)

An independent illustration uses exactly quadratic data, $\rho=\rho_0+BT^2$. If the trial residual differs by $\epsilon=\rho_r-\rho_0$, then $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=2BT^2/(BT^2-\epsilon)$. One hundred and eight finite-difference checks verify this relation. With the correct residual, a positive mixture of linear and quadratic terms gives $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=(A+2BT)/(A+BT)$, continuously between one and two; nine parameter cases confirm that limit. No abrupt phase boundary is required by this smooth crossover. These are synthetic checks, not fits to a paper's measurements. [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225)

Two additional synthetic fits use $\rho=10+0.1T^2$ in arbitrary consistent units. A best straight line over $9\leq T\leq11$ has maximum deviation below 0.4% of the mean resistivity; extending the interval to $1\leq T\leq20$ raises it above 20%. Thus apparent linearity over a narrow interval can hide curvature. These percentages depend on the chosen example and are not universal acceptance thresholds. Real analysis needs residual plots, independent temperature calibration and a measurement-specific noise model. [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225)

For parallel conducting channels, conductivities add: $\sigma_{\mathrm{dc}}=\sigma_0+S/\Gamma$ in a specified Drude convention. If $S$ and $\sigma_0$ are constant and $\Gamma\propto T$, the resulting resistivity is $\rho=1/(a+b/T)$, which is generally nonlinear. Adding channel resistivities would describe a different circuit. Temperature-dependent spectral weight and finite frequency coverage must also be tested before assigning a lifetime from the total d.c. curve. [R6](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

## Experimental criteria

Grissonnanche and colleagues measured angle-dependent magnetoresistance in Nd-LSCO at hole concentration $p=0.24\pm0.005$. The sample's zero-resistance transition is 11 K; the transport measurements use 45 T at 6, 12, 20 and 25 K. At the two lower temperatures, some field orientations still enter a superconducting region. A high field alone therefore does not certify that every angular data point is normal-state transport. [R4](#ref-source-374c8bc13ced83e8)

Their fit separates a temperature-independent anisotropic contribution from an isotropic temperature-dependent rate, giving $\alpha=1.2\pm0.4$ in $\hbar/\tau_{\mathrm{inel}}=\alpha k_BT$. Total scattering is not isotropic. The analysis uses a bandwidth scale $160\pm30$ meV constrained by specific heat and omits a low-temperature correction that could change it by about 20% at 6 K. Temperature stability of ±1 mK differs from absolute uncertainty of approximately ±1 K at 6 and 12 K. These qualifications belong with the extracted coefficient. [R4](#ref-source-374c8bc13ced83e8)

Romero and colleagues resolve a narrower Drude channel and a broad background in two FeTe1−xSex films. The final abstract associates most condensate spectral weight with the narrower channel. In the checked author version, the scattering-rate fits use 20–40 K, and linewidths below the approximately 0.2 THz measurement limit cannot be resolved directly. Its frequency variable uses cycles per second, so $h$ appears in $\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\alpha k_BT/h$; an angular-frequency rate instead uses $\hbar$. A fitted spectral decomposition is stronger evidence than assigning a lifetime from total resistance alone, but it does not uniquely determine the pairing interaction. [R6](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

Cooper and colleagues report that a low-temperature linear contribution extends over a range of LSCO dopings and tracks superconductivity. The checked abstract supports that association, not a reanalysis of its numerical curves. Magic-angle graphene measurements provide another material setting with nearly linear transport; a similar temperature exponent across systems does not prove identical scattering or pairing mechanisms. Material geometry, filling and fit assumptions must accompany cross-system comparisons. [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225) [R5](#ref-source-8135909144b6b16b)

## Limits and disputes

The marginal-Fermi-liquid proposal organizes anomalies using an assumed charge- and spin-response spectrum. Its phenomenological success would not identify a unique microscopic origin. The original article has an erratum; this review records the correction's identity but has not independently read its contents, and therefore does not reproduce a supposedly corrected derivation. Quantum critical, incoherent-metal and phonon-based explanations require separate discriminating evidence rather than a choice made from one resistivity exponent. [R7](#ref-source-5880a61b06ec84e6) [R8](#ref-source-2cdc898b9c5e504c) [R4](#ref-source-374c8bc13ced83e8)

Complete scientific acceptance remains open. Selected final passages and figures have been checked, but full version comparisons and raw-data fits remain incomplete. A slope correlation with superconductivity does not establish causation, and a channel contributing to the condensate does not identify the interaction binding its electrons. Retain alternative explanations and distinguish an experimental observable from quantities inferred through a model. [R2](#ref-source-1610fd228e679225) [R6](#ref-source-05b2d5ff6757b5eb)

## Discovery implications

Compare transport, spectroscopy and superconducting response on well-characterized samples, with field, geometry and temperature windows recorded. Use independent changes in disorder, pressure or filling to test whether a proposed scattering process also controls pairing. A useful discovery claim predicts an observable change and survives alternative fits; simply finding another straight resistivity curve adds a candidate observation, not a complete microscopic explanation.
