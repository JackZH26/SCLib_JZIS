## Scope

Ferromagnetic fluctuations describe enhanced magnetic response near zero momentum transfer. They can support odd-parity pairing in specified models, but neither a large uniform susceptibility nor a large upper critical field identifies a superconducting spin state. Magnetic order, correlations, gap symmetry and the pairing interaction are separate questions. Fay and Appel calculated equal-spin p-state pairing from longitudinal fluctuations in an itinerant-electron model; this is a theoretical possibility, not a universal material classification. ## Mathematical assumptions

For a simple effective exchange $V=-J\boldsymbol{\sigma}_1\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_2$ with $J>0$, the spin operator has eigenvalues $-3$ in the singlet and $+1$ in the triplet sector. The corresponding interaction eigenvalues are $+3J$ and $-J$. This independent two-spin illustration assumes a ferromagnetic effective interaction; it does not derive a material's exchange constant or its frequency dependence.

For even-frequency, single-orbital pairing, Fermi antisymmetry requires $\Delta(\mathbf k)=-\Delta^T(-\mathbf k)$. Writing a triplet matrix as $\Delta=(\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)i\sigma_y$ gives a symmetric spin matrix, so $\mathbf d(-\mathbf k)=-\mathbf d(\mathbf k)$. Odd parity here follows from these assumptions. Strong spin–orbit coupling requires a suitable pseudospin description; additional orbital or frequency structure changes this elementary argument.

A second independent illustration uses a circular two-dimensional Fermi surface and an attractive angular kernel $V(\theta)=-g/(a-b\cos\theta)$, with $g>0$ an energy scale and dimensionless $a>b>0$. Its dimensionless Fourier coefficients are

$$C_\ell=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\frac{\cos(\ell\theta)}{a-b\cos\theta}=\frac{r^\ell}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}},\qquad r=\frac{a-\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{b},\quad \ell\geq0.$$

The odd-parity projection $[V(\theta)-V(\theta+\pi)]/2$ retains odd angular harmonics. Since $0<r<1$, the strongest attractive odd harmonic in this example is $\ell=1$. Numerical quadrature checks the coefficients and projection. Crystal anisotropy, competing interactions and self-energy are absent: this example neither selects a universal p-wave state nor predicts a transition temperature.

## Experimental criteria

UGe2 superconductivity was reported in a restricted pressure range within an itinerant-ferromagnetic setting. URhGe supplied ambient-pressure thermodynamic evidence for coexistence; UCoGe supplied ambient-pressure superconducting and ferromagnetic signatures. These primary abstracts support the historical observations, while microscopic volume overlap and a particular gap representation require more than proximity in a phase diagram. Corrected original links replace two inherited citation errors. [R2](#ref-source-db2917242488a101) [R3](#ref-source-15aec45a868e9fb9) [R5](#ref-source-a4695ba5bfa789f3)

Lévy and colleagues reported a second superconducting pocket in URhGe around spin-axis reorientation near 12 T for field parallel to the crystal b axis, after low-field superconductivity had already disappeared. This is an orientation-specific re-entrant phenomenon. The original abstract suggests a connection to spin rotation; it does not uniquely establish a microscopic pairing kernel. [R4](#ref-source-e917adb251d93c59)

Pustogow and colleagues' 2019 Sr2RuO4 author manuscript reports a reduced oxygen-17 Knight shift and strong pulse-energy sensitivity. Its unstrained example uses an in-plane b-axis field of 0.7107 T; 20 mK is the base temperature, not a transition temperature. Selected methods explain why RF heating and different electronic/nuclear relaxation times can make a relaxation check insufficient. These observations challenge older unchanged-susceptibility interpretations; this entry does not infer a unique order parameter from them. [R6](#ref-source-c7f8b99a795aba41)

UTe2's discovery paper reported unusual anisotropic critical fields in a paramagnetic normal state. Its page 2 distinguishes a highest measured field of 20 T from an estimated zero-temperature b-axis value of 40 T. The estimate is not a measured endpoint. Historical triplet and ferromagnetic-pairing interpretations remain interpretations; the paper's schematic does not establish one continuous phase diagram shared by different uranium compounds. [R7](#ref-source-ca6322be2694e430)

Record field direction, pressure, composition, purity and temperature for each probe. Separate orbital/background contributions and pulse heating in Knight shifts; retain coupling, g-factor, multiband and orbital-limiting assumptions in critical-field comparisons. Combine symmetry-sensitive measurements with magnetic and superconducting volume evidence from the same sample. A bulk signal alone does not identify the mediating fluctuation.

## Limits and disputes

In UTe2, Duan and colleagues reported a neutron resonance associated with antiferromagnetic wavevectors. Their extended-data comparison found no ferromagnetic signal above background in the stated measurement window. This is not absence of every possible small-momentum fluctuation at every field, energy and temperature; a resonance coupled to superconductivity also does not alone prove the direction of causation. [R8](#ref-source-df199ac9453d6985)

A study published on 29 April 2026 measured UTe2 magnetotropic response in pulsed fields up to 60 T at 4 K, where the studied superconducting phases were suppressed. It inferred enhanced transverse, near-zero-momentum susceptibility at high fields. Here transverse means perpendicular to the applied field, not an off-diagonal tensor component. These normal-state measurements differ from zero/low-field neutron conditions. The displayed 300 mK superconducting boundaries came from earlier work, and 4 K must not be recorded as this experiment's superconducting transition. [R9](#ref-source-15cfc4a2f9587af0)

Fluctuations can enhance attraction while also renormalizing quasiparticles and affecting pair breaking. Fay–Appel's model gives a maximum followed by a decrease of calculated transition temperature toward the magnetic transition. Neither that model nor the elementary angular projection proves a universal optimum at a ferromagnetic quantum critical point. Longitudinal/transverse spectral weight, spin–orbit coupling and band structure must be specified. [R1](#ref-source-82c04c9061dbca4c)

## Discovery implications

Treat small-momentum magnetic response as a candidate-screening observation. Test whether a proposed kernel simultaneously explains symmetry constraints, normal-state responses and field/pressure trends under matched conditions. Keep negative observations and alternative channels. The recent UTe2 comparison illustrates why distinct experiments should retain their conditions instead of being reduced to a single ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic label. Selected source passages and model checks here leave full scientific acceptance open.
