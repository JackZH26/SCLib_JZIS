## Scope

p-wave describes orbital structure: angular momentum $l=1$ in a rotationally invariant continuum, or specified odd basis functions in a crystal. It is not a synonym for every odd-parity state. Spin triplet, odd parity, orbital representation and chirality answer different questions. The familiar relation between triplet and odd momentum parity assumes even-frequency pairing with no additional antisymmetric orbital index. Multiorbital and odd-frequency cases require the full exchange symmetry. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

Neutral liquid helium-3 provides the established triplet p-wave benchmark through its A and B phases. It does not certify a crystal superconductor by analogy. Sr2RuO4 remains a constrained, unresolved order-parameter problem, while uranium superconductors motivate triplet and odd-parity studies without thereby selecting a p-wave representation. ## Mathematical assumptions

For an even-frequency, single-band spin basis, write $\Delta(\mathbf k)=i[\mathbf d(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma]\sigma_y$, so $\Delta_{\uparrow\uparrow}=-d_x+id_y$, $\Delta_{\downarrow\downarrow}=d_x+id_y$ and $\Delta_{\uparrow\downarrow}=\Delta_{\downarrow\uparrow}=d_z$. Odd $\mathbf d(-\mathbf k)=-\mathbf d(\mathbf k)$ makes $\Delta(\mathbf k)=-\Delta^T(-\mathbf k)$, as required by fermionic exchange. In a centrosymmetric spin-orbit-coupled system the analogous pseudospin basis must be defined explicitly; these entries are not automatically laboratory spin populations. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

The identity $\Delta\Delta^\dagger=|\mathbf d|^2I+i(\mathbf d\times\mathbf d^*)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ gives squared gaps $|\mathbf d|^2\pm|i\mathbf d\times\mathbf d^*|$. A unitary state has vanishing cross product; it need not preserve time reversal. Four complex examples verify the matrix identity and exchange symmetry. Independent diagonalization of 48 imposed-gap Bogoliubov matrices also agrees with $E_\pm^2=\xi^2+|\mathbf d|^2\pm|i\mathbf d\times\mathbf d^*|$. The normal band here is spin-degenerate: this spectrum is not a general formula for a ferromagnetic multiband metal. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

On a unit circular Fermi contour, with gap scale one, compare polar $\mathbf d=(0,0,k_x)$, helical $\mathbf d=(k_x,k_y,0)$ and chiral $\mathbf d=(0,0,k_x+ik_y)$. Polar pairing has two nodes; the other two are fully gapped on this contour. All three are unitary. A global-phase comparison with the time-reversed texture preserves the polar and helical states but distinguishes the two chiral partners. Thus a full gap or unitary pairing alone does not establish broken time reversal. These are imposed examples, not self-consistent material solutions. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

Changing the Fermi surface changes the answer. On a sphere the same chiral form has squared gap $k_x^2+k_y^2=\sin^2\theta$ and two polar point nodes, whereas the illustrative BW texture $\mathbf d=(k_x,k_y,k_z)$ has constant squared gap one. Four spherical checks confirm both. Conversely, the nonunitary circular texture $\mathbf d=(k_x,ik_y,0)$ has squared branches $(|k_x|\pm|k_y|)^2$; a complex vector does not by itself imply the fully gapped chiral example above. No topological invariant is calculated here. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f) [R4](#ref-source-b4553aaaeacb3c3d) [R5](#ref-source-24556d6dba4d8cc1)

For a clean unitary model at zero temperature with negligible spin-orbit coupling and a fixed d texture, the ideal diagonal response is $\chi_{ii}/\chi_N=1-\langle|d_i|^2/|\mathbf d|^2\rangle$. Uniform circular averaging gives $(1/2,1/2,1)$ for the helical example; fixed-z chiral pairing gives $(1,1,0)$. Isotropic spherical BW averaging gives $(2/3,2/3,2/3)$. These omit interaction corrections, orbital response and field-driven rotation. They illustrate why a decrease for one field direction alone cannot identify a singlet or an orbital representation. ## Experimental criteria

The 1972 discovery record needs two steps. Osheroff, Richardson and Lee initially interpreted pressure anomalies in a Pomeranchuk cell as a possible new solid-helium phase. The later NMR paper, also authored by Gully, separated solid and liquid signals using a field gradient and associated the changes with the liquid. Its temperatures below 3 mK came from approximate melting-curve extrapolation. These measurements are historical evidence for new liquid phases, not a standalone determination of the full p-wave order parameter. [R2](#ref-source-017750e49092dcb4) [R3](#ref-source-b4c5ef61b3180334)

Leggett's later review develops the ABM and BW descriptions associated with A and B phases. Its 1976 erratum withdraws a claimed factor-of-two disagreement with Brinkman and Smith near the transition; it does not retract the phase framework. The review abstract and correction text are checked here, while its complete derivations remain outside this scoped review. Helium is neutral and approximately rotationally invariant, so its response cannot be transferred directly to a charged crystal with strong spin-orbit coupling. [R4](#ref-source-b4553aaaeacb3c3d) [R5](#ref-source-24556d6dba4d8cc1)

In Sr2RuO4, Pustogow and colleagues' oxygen-17 NMR work found a reduced spin response after controlling radio-frequency heating. The reviewed author manuscript distinguishes zero-strain measurements at 0.7107 T from strained measurements at 1.9980 T. This challenges the simple fixed-d-vector chiral interpretation used previously; it does not uniquely establish d-wave or invalidate every report of time-reversal breaking. Heating, field direction, strain and the assumed pseudospin response must accompany the inference. [R6](#ref-source-c7f8b99a795aba41)

Matsuki and colleagues' study was published on 9 February 2026; the checked author manuscript is dated December 2025. Comparing one and six crystals exposed stray-field artifacts. They combined single-crystal muon shifts with field-cooled magnetization of that same crystal. For fields along [100], they inferred a spin-shift reduction consistent with singlet-like behavior. The spin-contact extraction was insufficiently accurate at 0.2 T; magnetization stopped at 0.5 K. Their conclusions therefore do not distinguish fully gapped, line-nodal, helical and interorbital states. The reported 0.05 K spectrum is a measurement temperature, not a transition. [R7](#ref-source-c4434644191d0174)

Junction phase tests, directional spin response, thermodynamic gaps and symmetry-breaking probes should constrain the same proposed state under matched sample conditions. A time-reversal-breaking signal alone does not select p-wave over other complex order parameters or establish its microscopic origin. Boundary features require comparison with surface disorder and ordinary bound states, plus an independently specified bulk phase; calling a feature “Majorana-like” is not a pairing-symmetry measurement. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

## Limits and disputes

UGe2, URhGe and UCoGe have original reports connecting superconductivity with ferromagnetism under different pressure and sample conditions. These motivate equal-spin and odd-parity hypotheses, but ferromagnetic coexistence alone does not select a p-wave basis. Their source checks here are abstract-scoped; no new sample transition values or microscopic coexistence maps are extracted. [R8](#ref-source-db2917242488a101) [R9](#ref-source-15aec45a868e9fb9) [R10](#ref-source-a4695ba5bfa789f3)

The UTe2 discovery paper presents triplet-related evidence and a nearly ferromagnetic interpretation without ambient-pressure long-range ferromagnetic order. Its approximately 40 T b-axis critical-field estimate was extrapolated beyond measurements reaching 20 T. Treat that historical inference separately from a directly observed boundary or a settled orbital representation. Strong coupling, spin-orbit coupling, anisotropic orbital limiting and multiple bands must be considered when interpreting unusually large critical fields. [R11](#ref-source-ca6322be2694e430)

Inversion absence permits singlet–triplet mixing; multiple orbitals and spin-orbit coupling further change the bookkeeping. Even in an inversion-symmetric crystal, several odd basis functions can belong to the same representation. A label such as p-wave must state which basis and symmetry operations are meant. A finite collection of supporting observations is not a universal exclusion of all alternatives. [R1](#ref-source-8d1b859d5ffcad9f)

## Discovery implications

For each candidate, record inversion symmetry, the spin or pseudospin convention, field orientation, sample conditions and competing gap structures. Separate evidence for odd parity from evidence for chirality, nodes and topology. Use the explicit examples above to identify measurements that actually distinguish hypotheses. Full scientific acceptance, complete final-version comparisons and raw-data review remain open; the local model checks do not determine a material's pairing state.
