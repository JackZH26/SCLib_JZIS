## Scope

A pseudogap denotes suppressed low-energy spectral weight without requiring a fully gapped insulator or coherent superconductivity. In cuprates the term connects observations from nuclear magnetic resonance, photoemission, tunnelling and transport, but those probes interrogate different responses. A reported $T^*$ must retain its material, doping, observable and onset criterion. It need not be one universal transition temperature shared by every probe. [R1](#ref-source-2ecda5a1c9258a07) [R2](#ref-source-ce431fada9b3a183) [R3](#ref-source-b8f71a71e90d00ff)

The central question is which microscopic processes produce these changes and how they relate to superconductivity. Pairing, charge or magnetic order and strong-correlation scenarios remain distinguishable explanations. Observing a gap above $T_c$, a charge modulation or a Hall anomaly does not independently identify a unique origin. The original source chapter is retained, but its misattribution of Wise's STM sample to Na-CCOC is corrected here to Pb/La-substituted Bi2201. ## Mathematical assumptions

A useful non-uniqueness example is the two-level matrix $H=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&\Delta\\\Delta&-\xi\end{pmatrix}$ with real $\Delta$. Its diagonal retarded resolvent is $G_{11}(z)=(z+\xi)/(z^2-\xi^2-\Delta^2)$, where $z=\omega+i\eta$ and $\eta>0$. The spectral function is $A_{11}=-\operatorname{Im}G_{11}/\pi$. Its poles lie at $E_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\xi^2+\Delta^2}$, with positive weights $(1\pm\xi/\sqrt{\xi^2+\Delta^2})/2$. Three hundred fifteen numerical comparisons verify the resolvent against this pole decomposition; four finite-window integrations recover almost all spectral weight, with small Lorentzian tails outside the window. [R3](#ref-source-b8f71a71e90d00ff) [R7](#ref-source-4fa5e96f6d9eec1b)

In a perfectly nested density-wave illustration, the matrix acts on two electron momenta; in a BCS illustration it acts on a particle–hole Nambu basis. The same diagonal spectrum can therefore accompany physically different off-diagonal correlations. This independently checked example does not model an actual cuprate, establish nesting or calculate phase stiffness. It shows why a spectral gap alone cannot select the broken symmetry. Additional momentum, spin, phase-sensitive and thermodynamic information must constrain the interpretation. [R3](#ref-source-b8f71a71e90d00ff) [R7](#ref-source-4fa5e96f6d9eec1b)

For the Hall comparison, $n_H=V/(eR_H)$ expresses a number per chosen structural unit when $V$ is the corresponding volume. Its interpretation as carrier density requires transport assumptions; multiple bands, mobility imbalance, Fermi-surface reconstruction and scattering anisotropy can alter $R_H$. The high-field inference is consequently stronger when accompanied by magnetoresistance and specimen calibration, and it cannot be transferred unchanged to zero-field spectra. [R6](#ref-source-fae77f2c38d4cf72)

## Experimental criteria

Warren's YBa2Cu3O6.7 study reports a decrease of planar Cu nuclear relaxation below about 100 K, above its bulk superconducting transition. The authors discuss spin pairing as a possibility; the abstract does not establish Cooper-pair phase coherence. Alloul's 89Y NMR work separately constrains spin susceptibility through shifts and relaxation. Ding's underdoped Bi2212 ARPES reports an anisotropic normal-state pseudogap evolving into the superconducting gap. These historical results are checked at primary-abstract scope here, without reproducing their lineshape or calibration analyses. [R1](#ref-source-2ecda5a1c9258a07) [R2](#ref-source-ce431fada9b3a183) [R3](#ref-source-b8f71a71e90d00ff)

Wise's final paper measures Pb/La-substituted Bi2201. Its Figures 1–2 compare conductance maps at 6 K: specimens labelled by $T_c=35,32,25$ K have modulation periods approximately $6.2,5.1,4.5a_0$, respectively. The wavelength varies with doping, while the reported modulation is nondispersive over the investigated bias window. That supports the authors' charge-order interpretation within these measurements; it neither proves a universal pseudogap mechanism nor assigns their data to Ca2−xNaxCuO2Cl2, which appears as cited earlier work. The 6 K mapping temperature is not a transition. [R4](#ref-source-07213acd01bb9b90)

Rullier-Albenque's YBCO analysis uses pulsed fields up to 55–60 T along the c axis and a normal-state magnetoresistance background to extract excess conductivity. The author v2 defines a fluctuation-onset threshold of $10^3\,(\Omega\,\mathrm{m})^{-1}$; the earlier v1 prints $10^{-3}$ at that location, so version choice matters. Figure 8 shows distinct doping trends for the fluctuation onset and pseudogap scale. This constrains the identification of the entire pseudogap region with superconducting fluctuations. The threshold is an operational sensitivity criterion, not a universal thermodynamic boundary. [R5](#ref-source-377dc0bc5c5160fa)

Badoux's Hall measurements reach 88 T. The author Figure 4 compares YBCO samples at 50 K using values measured or extrapolated to 80 T, with about 15% uncertainty in the absolute Hall coefficient. The inferred change near $p^*=0.19\pm0.01$ is separated from the charge-reconstruction endpoint near $p=0.16$. Those values belong to this material analysis. The two highest dopings use Ca substitution, and the figure also incorporates comparison data from other cuprates; it must not be presented as one homogeneous specimen series or an unconditional carrier count. [R6](#ref-source-fae77f2c38d4cf72)

## Limits and disputes

Sordi's calculation obtains a pseudogap and a nearby Widom crossover in a square-lattice Hubbard model using a 2 × 2 plaquette CDMFT approximation at $U=6.2t$. Spectral, spin and singlet-probability criteria approach the compressibility crossover near the model's critical endpoint. Competing broken symmetries are unnecessary for that calculation to produce a pseudogap, but this does not exclude their role in real materials. The plotted conversion using $t=0.35$ eV is a model choice, not a universal experimental temperature calibration. [R7](#ref-source-4fa5e96f6d9eec1b)

Magnetic interpretations also remain contested. Fauqué reports translation-preserving polarized-neutron magnetic order; Croft reports no corresponding order in two underdoped YBCO specimens. Croft's upper bound depends on the assumed current pattern and experimental sensitivity. The linked 2018 Comment and Reply are recorded as identities, not as a dispute resolved by this review. Neither one positive signal nor one null result establishes the origin of every pseudogap observation. Complete scientific acceptance remains open. Historical abstracts, selected figures and toy spectra do not replace full final-version comparisons, raw-data reproduction or a comprehensive competing-literature assessment. Maintain separate definitions for a gap magnitude, crossover onset, charge modulation and superconducting transition. ## Discovery implications

Seek predictions that discriminate between explanations on characterized specimens. Compare the same doping and field conditions across spectroscopy, transport and symmetry probes; test whether disorder or controlled tuning separates pairing fluctuations from the larger spectral suppression. A promising theory should explain both supporting observations and boundary cases instead of treating every normal-state anomaly as its confirmation.
