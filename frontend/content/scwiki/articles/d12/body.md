## Scope

Topological superconductivity concerns the superconducting quasiparticle state, not merely a topological normal-state band next to a superconducting transition. Candidate platforms include intrinsically paired crystals, proximitized surfaces and engineered semiconductor devices. This comparison separates measured superconductivity, a proposed topological phase and evidence for localized Majorana modes. Their criteria and relevant dimensionality differ; a surface result does not automatically classify an entire three-dimensional bulk. [R1](#ref-source-37986d2488e4cace) [R2](#ref-source-4e92d11d59fb3441) [R3](#ref-source-7dc64372df6ba886) [R8](#ref-source-e2865b9f2461bc7f)

For a fully gapped Bogoliubov–de Gennes description, topology depends on dimensionality, symmetries and the occupied quasiparticle states. This restricted framework does not exhaust interacting or nodal superconductivity. A local zero-bias peak is neither a universal requirement for every topological superconductor nor sufficient proof of a Majorana mode. Demonstrating non-Abelian operations is a further question; it is not the definition of every bulk topological superconducting phase. ## Mathematical assumptions

One clean single-subband wire model uses $H(k)=\xi_k\tau_z+\alpha k\tau_z\sigma_y+V_Z\sigma_x+\Delta\tau_x$, where $\xi_k=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)-\mu$, $\sigma$ acts on spin and $\tau$ on particle–hole space. Here $\Delta$ is real induced pairing, spin–orbit and Zeeman axes are perpendicular, and $V_Z=|g\mu_B B|/2$ is an energy rather than a magnetic field. With $a=\alpha k$, diagonalization yields $E_\pm^2=\xi_k^2+a^2+V_Z^2+\Delta^2\pm2\sqrt{V_Z^2(\xi_k^2+\Delta^2)+\xi_k^2a^2}$. [R4](#ref-source-45c980d0afc5ac14) [R5](#ref-source-87a91e2d8cd79e95)

The zero-momentum gap closes at $V_Z^2=\mu^2+\Delta^2$. In the stated gapped wire model, the side with $V_Z^2>\mu^2+\Delta^2$ supports the topological phase. The inequality alone is not a material diagnostic: setting spin–orbit coupling to zero can leave finite-momentum gapless excitations even when it holds. Independent checks compare 54 matrix spectra, three closure points and that counterexample. They do not simulate multiband disorder, orbital depairing, interfaces or a complete material invariant. [R4](#ref-source-45c980d0afc5ac14) [R5](#ref-source-87a91e2d8cd79e95)

The Fu–Berg criterion assumes a three-dimensional, fully gapped, time-reversal-invariant BdG state with inversion symmetry and odd-parity pairing. Its normal-state Fermi-surface shortcut also assumes weak pairing and bands separated from the Fermi energy at the time-reversal-invariant momenta (TRIM).

- **Use the BdG inversion operator:** odd-parity pairing requires $\widetilde P=P\tau_z$. The invariant multiplies its negative-energy eigenvalues at the eight TRIM, taking one member of each Kramers pair. Multiplying both members would square each sign and lose the parity information.
- **Read the result as a sufficient test:** the parity $\nu=n\bmod2$ is only part of the three-dimensional integer classification. An odd result establishes nonzero $n$ within these assumptions. An even result does not by itself establish $n=0$; an even, nonzero invariant is not excluded. Counting enclosed TRIM neither measures the pairing parity nor certifies a doped specimen.
- **Keep surface and vortex states separate:** Fu–Kane's uniform proximitized helical surface has a gapped spectrum and preserves time reversal, despite its projected analogy with spinless $p_x+ip_y$ pairing. A vortex introduces a winding pairing phase, $\Delta(r,\theta)=\Delta_0(r)e^{\pm i\theta}$, and admits a localized zero mode in that ideal model. The vortex calculation is not an observation of a zero mode on every uniform surface, nor a demonstration of braiding.

These are different theoretical constructions. Applying either to a material requires checking its own symmetry, gap, geometry and disorder conditions; neither theorem replaces those measurements. [R2](#ref-source-4e92d11d59fb3441) [R3](#ref-source-7dc64372df6ba886)

## Experimental criteria

Hor's Cu-intercalated Bi2Se3 study reports a transition near 3.8 K. In the checked author manuscript, Cu0.12Bi2Se3 has a magnetic response roughly one-fifth of ideal diamagnetism at the lowest accessible temperature, which the authors treat as a lower bound because the transition is incomplete. The resistance does not reach zero. This establishes why sample preparation and connected superconducting paths matter; neither a universal superconducting volume nor topological pairing can be inferred from those numbers alone. [R6](#ref-source-0857b567ea17c2f2)

Butch's YPtBi crystals show a resistive transition near 0.77 K with an AC diamagnetic response. The critical-field curve uses a 50% resistive criterion, with 10–90% bounds, and field perpendicular to [001]. The often-quoted 1.5 T is a zero-temperature extrapolation. Converting it with $B_{c2}=\Phi_0/(2\pi\xi^2)$ gives about 14.8 nm as a consistency estimate. Superconductivity in a candidate topological semimetal does not itself identify its pairing topology or establish a calibrated superconducting volume. [R7](#ref-source-4d53a5f7cdff4a54)

Two iron-chalcogenide papers require separate attribution. Zhang's ARPES study resolves a spin-helical surface band and a superconducting surface gap around 1.8 meV, interpreted as surface topological superconductivity. Its low-temperature measurement at 2.4 K is not the reported 14.5 K transition temperature. The study's surface interpretation must not be replaced by a statement that all bulk pairing is topological. In its angular-gap figure, hollow mirrored markers are not additional independent measurements. [R8](#ref-source-e2865b9f2461bc7f)

Wang's distinct vortex-STM paper studies FeTe0.55Se0.45. Figure 2 spectra at 0.55 K and 0.5 T parallel to c are normalized by integrated spectral area; they cannot be read as an absolute quantized conductance plateau. The approximately 0.3 meV peak width contains instrumental and thermal broadening. Barrier and temperature scans elsewhere use different fields. The zero-energy signal is interpreted as Majorana evidence, while the displayed braiding sequence is a proposal, not a performed operation. [R9](#ref-source-a972be8eb742181f)

Xu's Bi2Te3/NbSe2 heterostructure study compares vortex spectra across film thicknesses. The checked 2013 author version underlying the 2015 publication distinguishes where split peaks become experimentally resolvable from the extrapolated origin of their splitting. The former is around 20 nm even for thinner films; the latter changes near four quintuple layers and supports the authors' Majorana interpretation. Treating every unsplit interval as a directly measured Majorana radius would discard both the extrapolation and approximately 0.2 meV resolution. [R10](#ref-source-6861a8084ed315a1)

Semiconductor readout advances also need their own evidence category. Aghaee's 2025 InAs–Al experiment measures single-shot parity interferometrically and explicitly retains an Andreev-state alternative. Van Loo's 2026 work demonstrates parity readout in a minimal Kitaev-chain device, with a two-site rather than asymptotically long-chain model. These are useful experimental capabilities; neither result, as scoped here, establishes braiding or fault-tolerant topological computation. Readout performance, spatial nonlocality and a protected bulk phase remain distinct questions. [R14](#ref-source-e3824a8727c3ec54) [R15](#ref-source-a3c2dc153700478a)

A 10 July 2026 preprint by Zatelli and colleagues reports coherent parity oscillations in coupled minimal Kitaev chains. The authors distinguish partial protection at the sweet spot from the unprotected degeneracy-lifting operations used for rotation. No journal acceptance was verified here; this is not a demonstrated braid or a certified gate fidelity. [R16](#ref-source-818818441532a8ec)

## Limits and disputes

Confinement, disorder and ordinary Andreev states can produce misleading local zero-bias signatures. Even apparent agreement with an ideal conductance value needs temperature, barrier, calibration and nonlocal checks. The 2018 paper *Quantized Majorana conductance* was retracted in 2021; its original result is retained here only as retracted history. The notice describes charge-jump corrections, an axis-label problem and conductance recalibration. A withdrawn claim cannot count as independent confirmation of later devices. [R11](#ref-source-dca781066e96eecc) [R12](#ref-source-6a7f18eec743e89d) [R13](#ref-source-f127bb2d99183698)

Complete scientific acceptance remains open. This article checks selected primary passages, figures and restricted models. Earlier author versions are identified; complete final-version comparisons, supplements, raw-data analysis, calibration and exclusion of competing interpretations remain unfinished. These limits concern the present review and should not be inflated into a claim that every platform has the same evidential status. ## Discovery implications

Specify the target first: bulk odd-parity superconductivity, a proximitized topological surface, a wire end state or a controllable parity device. Then record composition, geometry, temperature, field, superconducting criterion and competing-state tests for that target. Compare complementary probes on characterized samples and preserve failures and retractions. Strong spin–orbit coupling or an attractive band calculation can motivate an experiment, but cannot replace identification of the actual paired phase and its protected observables.
