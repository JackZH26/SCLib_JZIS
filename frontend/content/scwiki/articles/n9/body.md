## Scope

Topological superconductivity and a high transition temperature answer different questions. Topology classifies a specified superconducting quasiparticle state under stated symmetries and dimensionality; Tc concerns the establishment of superconducting order under specified conditions. A topological label alone supplies neither a pairing interaction nor a temperature scale. Conversely, a large gap or high Tc does not determine a topological invariant. An explicit fixed-pairing example below separates those statements without claiming a microscopic theory of any material. [R1](#ref-source-ade538b429fdd847) [R2](#ref-source-7dc64372df6ba886)

Distinguish a three-dimensional bulk superconducting phase, superconductivity induced on a topological surface, and a topological vortex line with bound states at its ends. These can coexist without having the same classification. In the Fu–Kane construction, ordinary s-wave pairing induced on a single helical surface produces a state with vortex Majorana modes. Its relation to a spinless p-wave model does not mean that the uniform surface spontaneously breaks time reversal. [R2](#ref-source-7dc64372df6ba886) [R3](#ref-source-e584f37aa67e112b)

## Experimental criteria

Keep several measured scales separate: resistive onset and zero resistance; bulk and surface gaps; the lowest unwanted vortex excitation; spectral linewidth; and the temperature at which a particular signal remains detectable. Use matched compositions, thicknesses, fields and measurement protocols. A gap observed by photoemission and a peak observed by tunnelling are complementary evidence, not interchangeable thermometers or measurements of non-Abelian statistics. [R4](#ref-source-e2865b9f2461bc7f) [R5](#ref-source-a972be8eb742181f)

A zero-bias peak requires tests against alternative bound states and disorder. Such alternatives are explicitly demonstrated in theoretical nanowire studies, although their detailed conclusions cannot be transferred unchanged to an iron-based vortex. Establish the relevant band structure, pairing, spatial localization and controlled parameter dependence for the actual platform. A high material Tc does not remove these identification requirements. [R7](#ref-source-dca781066e96eecc)

## What iron-chalcogenide experiments establish

Zhang and colleagues examined FeTe0.55Se0.45 with a reported bulk Tc of 14.5 K. Their checked author version resolves a spin-helical surface band and an approximately isotropic surface gap near 1.8 meV. High-resolution photoemission and spin-resolved measurements have different stated energy resolutions, about 1.4 and 5.5 meV respectively. These observations support their proposed superconducting topological surface. They do not measure a braid operation, and the same article distinguishes the surface interpretation from its bulk superconducting state. [R4](#ref-source-e2865b9f2461bc7f)

Wang and colleagues measured vortex spectra in that composition at 0.55 K. In the checked author version, selected non-split peaks have a width around 0.3 meV, close to their combined thermal and instrument broadening estimate. Their temperature study finds that peak amplitudes fall strongly around 3 K and a selected peak is invisible at 4.2 K, despite the reported 14.5 K bulk Tc. The authors interpret the extra suppression in terms of quasiparticle poisoning. This separates a proposed mode's visibility from the superconducting transition; it is not a universal device operating limit. Their braiding illustration is a future proposal. [R5](#ref-source-a972be8eb742181f)

Lin and colleagues studied 10-unit-cell FeTexSe1−x films on SrTiO3. The final selected results report a superconducting surface state over part of a Te-rich composition range and its disappearance near the FeTe limit, alongside suppressed superconductivity. For x = 0.93, the approximately 13 K resistive onset and 7.9 K zero-resistance temperature are distinct. Their photoemission and many-body calculations connect orbital correlations with the changing bands and superconducting dome. This is evidence of a material-specific interplay, not proof that a topological transition universally raises Tc. The article appeared online in December 2025 and carries a 2026 volume designation. [R6](#ref-source-c54d271015976534)

## Mathematical assumptions

An original prescribed one-dimensional Kitaev-band example uses lattice spacing one and real parameters:

$$
H(k)=(-\mu-2t\cos k)\tau_z+2\Delta\sin k\,\tau_y,\qquad
E_\pm(k)=\pm\sqrt{(\mu+2t\cos k)^2+4\Delta^2\sin^2 k}.
$$

For nonzero pairing, positive hopping and a gapped chain, the parity invariant in this convention is

$$
Q=\operatorname{sgn}[(-\mu-2t)(-\mu+2t)].
$$

The nontrivial regime has Q = −1. Independent matrix diagonalization, phase winding and analytic gap minimization agree in 27 parameter cases. Multiplying t, μ and Δ by the same positive factor leaves eigenvectors and Q unchanged while scaling the excitation gap. Two additional cases verify gap closure at μ = ±2t, where the invariant is not assigned. Thus even this explicit topology does not fix an absolute gap scale. The calculation prescribes pairing; it does not solve its temperature dependence or predict Tc. [R1](#ref-source-ade538b429fdd847)

The ideal equilibrium thermal-resolution kernel is the derivative of the Fermi function:

$$
-\frac{df}{dE}=\frac{1}{4k_BT}\operatorname{sech}^2\!\left(\frac{E}{2k_BT}\right),\qquad
\mathrm{FWHM}=4\operatorname{arcosh}(\sqrt2)\,k_BT\simeq3.53k_BT.
$$

Four independent temperature cases verify the half-maximum relation. At 1 K this kernel alone has a width near 0.304 meV. Actual tunnelling spectra also involve intrinsic states, coupling, instrument response and potentially nonequilibrium effects. This example is not a fit to a measured peak and does not calculate poisoning or qubit fidelity. [R5](#ref-source-a972be8eb742181f)

## Why a larger Tc is not enough

Hosur and colleagues show that vortex-end modes can depend on a topological transition of the vortex line while bulk superconductivity persists. Their selected weak-pairing treatment assumes inversion- and time-reversal-symmetric normal bands; chemical potential and vortex orientation matter. The vortex minigap, parametrically Δ/(kFξ) in the stated limit, can be much smaller than the bulk gap. It cannot be replaced blindly by kBTc or extrapolated into a different strong-pairing regime. A normal-state surface band alone is therefore insufficient to determine every superconducting vortex's topology. [R3](#ref-source-e584f37aa67e112b)

## Limits and disputes

The evidence reviewed here does not establish a universal causal relation between topology and high Tc, or demonstrate a functioning high-temperature topological qubit. Selected author manuscripts are distinguished from fully compared final papers; raw spectra and complete supplements remain open. The Lin study's selected final HTML is checked, but its complete methods and figures were not obtained in this review. Proposed Majorana interpretations retain their measurement and competing-explanation limits. ## Discovery implications

A useful search should optimize several properties separately: a reproducible superconducting state, the desired topological phase, an isolating excitation gap, material uniformity and accessible control. Compare changes in topology with independently measured onset, zero resistance and surface gap rather than selecting the largest reported temperature. Study whether improved pairing strength preserves the required band and vortex conditions. Ultimately, controlled parity and exchange experiments must test device-level claims; neither a band calculation nor a larger Tc substitutes for them. These are proposed research priorities, not an accepted route to a guaranteed temperature increase.
